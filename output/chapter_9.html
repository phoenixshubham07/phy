A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q9.1: First Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]

State and explain the first law of thermodynamics. Define internal energy, heat, and work.

* First Law of Thermodynamics: The heat energy supplied to a system equals the sum of the change in internal energy and the work done by the system.

ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW

Or: ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV (for constant pressure)

Definitions:

1. Internal Energy (U):

• Total kinetic and potential energy of all molecules in the system
• Depends only on temperature (for ideal gas)
• State function (depends only on current state, not path)
• Increases with temperature

2. Heat (Q):

• Energy transferred due to temperature difference
• Positive (Q > 0): Heat absorbed by system
• Negative (Q < 0): Heat released by system
• Path function (depends on how energy is transferred)

3. Work (W):

• Energy transferred by mechanical means (expansion/compression)
• Positive (W > 0): Work done BY system (expansion)
• Negative (W < 0): Work done ON system (compression)
• W = PΔV for constant pressure

First Law Interpretation:

ΔQ = ΔU + W

• Heat input = Increase in internal energy + Work done by system
• Energy conservation: Total energy input must equal total output

Example Processes:

1. Isothermal (T = constant):

• ΔU = 0 (temperature constant)
• ΔQ = W (all heat converts to work)

2. Adiabatic (ΔQ = 0):

• 0 = ΔU + W
• ΔU = -W (work done reduces internal energy, temperature drops)

3. Isochoric (V = constant):

• W = 0 (no expansion)
• ΔQ = ΔU (all heat increases internal energy)

4. Isobaric (P = constant):

• W = PΔV
• ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV

Q9.2: Second Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]

State the second law of thermodynamics in two forms. Explain why perfect heat engines are impossible.

* Second Law - Kelvin-Planck Statement: It is impossible to construct an engine that operates in a cycle, producing only work and absorbing energy from a single thermal reservoir. Some heat must be rejected to a cold reservoir.

Implication: Heat cannot be completely converted to work in a cyclic process.

Second Law - Clausius Statement: It is impossible for heat to flow spontaneously from a cold body to a hot body without external work being done.

Implication: Heat flows naturally from hot to cold, never spontaneously reverse.

Why Perfect Heat Engines Are Impossible:

A "perfect" engine would:

• Take heat Qh from hot reservoir (at temperature Th)
• Convert ALL of it to useful work (W = Qh)
• Reject NO heat to cold reservoir (Qc = 0)

This violates the second law because: 1. Energy alone isn't sufficient:

• First law doesn't forbid this (ΔU = 0 for cycle)
• Energy is conserved, but it's thermodynamically forbidden

2. Entropy Argument:

• Heat extraction decreases hot reservoir's entropy: ΔSh = -Qh/Th
• No heat added to cold reservoir: ΔSc = 0
• Total entropy: ΔStotal = -Qh/Th < 0
• Entropy decreases, violating second law!
• Second law requires: ΔStotal ≥ 0

3. Maximum Efficiency (Carnot Limit): Actual efficiency: η = W/Qh = (Qh - Qc)/Qh = 1 - (Qc/Qh)

For Carnot engine (idealized, reversible): η_Carnot = 1 - Tc/Th

Even this perfect engine cannot achieve 100% efficiency:

• If Tc = 0 K: η = 100% (only theoretically)
• In practice: Tc > 0, so η < 1

Why All Real Engines Are Less Efficient: 1. Friction and dissipation 2. Irreversible processes 3. Non-ideal heat transfer 4. Pressure/temperature losses

Q9.3: First Law - Isothermal Process [5 marks]

One mole of an ideal gas at pressure 10 atm and volume 2 L undergoes isothermal expansion to 10 L at temperature 300 K. Find: (a) Work done by gas (b) Heat absorbed (c) Change in internal energy (d) Verify the first law

(1 atm = 101,325 Pa, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K))

*

Given:

• n = 1 mole
• T = 300 K (constant, isothermal)
• V₁ = 2 L = 2 × 10⁻³ m³
• V₂ = 10 L = 10 × 10⁻³ m³
• P₁ = 10 atm = 1,013,250 Pa

(a) Work Done by Gas:

For isothermal process: W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) W = 1 × 8.314 × 300 × ln(10/2) W = 2494.2 × ln(5) W = 2494.2 × 1.609 W = 4,012 J ≈ 4.01 kJ

Positive work indicates expansion (work done by gas).

(b) Heat Absorbed:

For ideal gas in isothermal process: ΔU = 0 (internal energy depends only on temperature, which is constant)

From first law: ΔQ = ΔU + W = 0 + 4,012 ΔQ = 4,012 J ≈ 4.01 kJ

Heat is absorbed by system (positive Q).

(c) Change in Internal Energy:

For isothermal process (T = constant): ΔU = 0 (no change in temperature)

(d) Verify First Law:

ΔQ = ΔU + W 4,012 = 0 + 4,012 4,012 = 4,012 ✓

Physical Interpretation:

• Gas expands at constant temperature
• All heat absorbed goes into doing work on surroundings
• No temperature change means no change in molecular kinetic energy
• This is an idealized process (perfect thermal contact with heat reservoir)

Q9.4: Heat Engine Efficiency [5 marks]

A heat engine operates between hot reservoir at 500 K and cold reservoir at 300 K. The engine absorbs 1000 J of heat per cycle from the hot reservoir. (a) Calculate maximum theoretical efficiency (Carnot efficiency) (b) If actual efficiency is 60% of Carnot efficiency, find actual efficiency (c) Find work output (d) Find heat rejected to cold reservoir

*

Given:

• Th = 500 K (hot reservoir)
• Tc = 300 K (cold reservoir)
• Qh = 1000 J (heat absorbed from hot reservoir)

(a) Carnot Efficiency (Maximum Possible):

η_Carnot = 1 - Tc/Th = 1 - 300/500 η_Carnot = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4 η_Carnot = 40% (maximum possible)

(b) Actual Efficiency:

η_actual = 0.6 × η_Carnot = 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.24 η_actual = 24%

(c) Work Output:

W = η_actual × Qh = 0.24 × 1000 W = 240 J (useful work output)

(d) Heat Rejected:

From first law (cyclic process, ΔU = 0): Qh = W + Qc Qc = Qh - W = 1000 - 240 Qc = 760 J (wasted heat)

Energy Flow:

• In: 1000 J from hot source
• Out: 240 J as work, 760 J as heat to cold source
• Efficiency: 240/1000 = 24%

Why Not Carnot Efficiency?

If actual engine operated at Carnot efficiency (40%):

• W = 0.40 × 1000 = 400 J
• Qc = 600 J

But the problem states efficiency is 60% of Carnot (60% of ideal), so:

• W = 240 J
• Qc = 760 J

This is more realistic (real engines are less efficient due to irreversibility).

Q9.5: Kinetic Theory of Gases [3 marks]

Write the equation for pressure of an ideal gas according to the Kinetic Theory.

* Pressure Equation: According to the kinetic theory, gas pressure arises from molecular collisions with container walls.

Formula: PV = (1/3) N m <v²>

Where:

• P = Pressure
• V = Volume
• N = Total number of molecules
• m = Mass of one molecule
• <v²> = Mean square velocity

This relates macroscopic pressure to microscopic molecular motion.

Q10.5: Speed of Sound & Laplace Correction [3 marks]

Write Newton's formula for the speed of sound in a gas and explain Laplace's correction.

* Newton's Formula: Assumed isothermal propagation: v = √(P/ρ) This gave a value (~280 m/s) much lower than experimental results for air (332 m/s).

Laplace's Correction: Laplace suggested that sound propagation is an adiabatic process (rapid changes, no heat exchange). Formula: v = √(γP/ρ) Where γ = Cp/Cv (adiabatic index). For air, γ ≈ 1.4. This corrected formula matches experimental values accurately.

Q10.6: Organ Pipes [5 marks]

Compare the fundamental frequencies of an open organ pipe and a closed organ pipe of the same length L.

* 1. Open Pipe (Open at both ends): Wavelength λ = 2L Frequency fopen = v / 2L Harmonics: All harmonics present (f, 2f, 3f...)

2. Closed Pipe (Closed at one end): Wavelength λ = 4L Frequency fclosed = v / 4L Harmonics: Only odd harmonics present (f, 3f, 5f...)

Comparison: fopen / fclosed = (v/2L) / (v/4L) = 2 The fundamental frequency of an open pipe is twice that of a closed pipe of the same length.

Q9.6: Mayer's Relation (Cp - Cv) [5 marks]

Derive the relation Cp - Cv = R for an ideal gas.

* Derivation: Consider 1 mole of ideal gas. First Law: ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW 1. Constant Volume Heating (ΔV=0): ΔQ = CvΔT ΔW = PΔV = 0 ΔU = CvΔT (This holds for any process for ideal gas)

2. Constant Pressure Heating: ΔQ = CpΔT ΔW = PΔV = P(RΔT/P) = RΔT (From PV=RT) Substitute into First Law: CpΔT = CvΔT + RΔT Divide by ΔT: Cp - Cv = R

Calculate the RMS speed of Nitrogen molecules at 300 K. (Molar mass of N₂ = 28 g/mol, R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

* Formula: vrms = √(3RT/M) Convert M to kg: M = 28 × 10⁻³ kg/mol T = 300 K
