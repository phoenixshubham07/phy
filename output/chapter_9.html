A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q9.1: First Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]

State and explain the first law of thermodynamics. Define internal energy, heat, and work.

* Concept: The First Law is just the "Law of Conservation of Energy" for heat. It says: "Energy cannot be destroyed." If you heat a gas (`Q`), the energy does two things: 1. Heats up the gas (Internal Energy `U` increases). 2. Pushes the piston out (Work `W` is done). So, Heat Input = Temp Rise + Work Done.

Formula: ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW

Sign Convention: * Heat Added (ΔQ): Positive * Work by System (ΔW): Positive (Expansion) * Internal Energy Increase (ΔU): Positive (Temp rise)

Solution: The heat energy supplied to a system equals the sum of the change in internal energy and the work done by the system. This can also be written as: ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV (for constant pressure)

Definitions:

• Negative (Q < 0): Heat released by system
• Path function (depends on how energy is transferred)

3. Work (W):

• Energy transferred by mechanical means (expansion/compression)
• Positive (W > 0): Work done BY system (expansion)
• Negative (W < 0): Work done ON system (compression)
• W = PΔV for constant pressure

First Law Interpretation:

ΔQ = ΔU + W

• Heat input = Increase in internal energy + Work done by system
• Energy conservation: Total energy input must equal total output

Example Processes:

1. Isothermal (T = constant):

• ΔU = 0 (temperature constant)
• ΔQ = W (all heat converts to work)

2. Adiabatic (ΔQ = 0):

• 0 = ΔU + W
• ΔU = -W (work done reduces internal energy, temperature drops)

3. Isochoric (V = constant):

• W = 0 (no expansion)
• ΔQ = ΔU (all heat increases internal energy)

4. Isobaric (P = constant):

• W = PΔV
• ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV

Q9.2: Second Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]

State the second law of thermodynamics in two forms. Explain why perfect heat engines are impossible.

* Concept: The Second Law is about "Real Life vs. Theory". * Heat Flow: Heat naturally flows Hot → Cold. You need a machine (fridge) to force it the other way. * Efficiency: You can't turn 100% of heat into work. Some heat is *always* wasted (dumped into the cold sink). That's why perpetual motion machines are impossible.

Statement: * Kelvin-Planck: No cyclic engine can convert heat completely into work. (Efficiency < 100%). * Clausius: Heat cannot flow spontaneously from cold to hot.

Why Impossible: A perfect engine implies ΔStotal < 0 for the universe, which violates the Entropy principle (ΔS ≥ 0).

Q9.3: First Law - Isothermal Process [5 marks]

One mole of an ideal gas at pressure 10 atm and volume 2 L undergoes isothermal expansion to 10 L at temperature 300 K. Find: (a) Work done by gas (b) Heat absorbed (c) Change in internal energy (d) Verify the first law

(1 atm = 101,325 Pa, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K))

* Concept: Isothermal means "Constant Temperature". * Since temperature doesn't change, the internal energy of the ideal gas stays the same (`ΔU = 0`). * Therefore, All the heat you put in (`Q`) is directly converted into Work (`W`). None is stored.

Formula: * W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) * ΔU = nCvΔT * ΔQ = ΔU + W

Calculation: Given: n=1, T=300, V₁=2L, V₂=10L.

(a) Work Done: W = (1)(8.314)(300) ln(10/2) W = 2494.2 × 1.609 = 4013 J

(b) Heat Absorbed: Since ΔU = 0, ΔQ = W = 4013 J

(c) Internal Energy: ΔU = 0 (Isothermal)

(d) Verification: 4013 = 0 + 4013 (First Law Holds).

Q9.4: Heat Engine Efficiency [5 marks]

A heat engine operates between hot reservoir at 500 K and cold reservoir at 300 K. The engine absorbs 1000 J of heat per cycle from the hot reservoir. (a) Calculate maximum theoretical efficiency (Carnot efficiency) (b) If actual efficiency is 60% of Carnot efficiency, find actual efficiency (c) Find work output (d) Find heat rejected to cold reservoir

* Concept: * Efficiency (η) is simply: "What percentage of the fuel (heat) actually turned into useful movement (work)?" * Carnot Efficiency: This is the absolute theoretical limit. No engine in the universe can be more efficient than a Carnot engine operating between the same two temperatures.

Formula: * η_carnot = 1 - Tc/Th * η = W / Qh * W = Qh - Qc

Calculation: (a) Carnot Efficiency: η = 1 - 300/500 = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4 (40%)

(b) Actual Efficiency: η_act = 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.24 (24%)

(c) Work Output: W = 0.24 × 1000 = 240 J

(d) Heat Rejected: Qc = Qh - W = 1000 - 240 = 760 J

Q9.5: Kinetic Theory of Gases [3 marks]

Write the equation for pressure of an ideal gas according to the Kinetic Theory.

* Concept: Why does gas have pressure? Because billions of tiny gas molecules are zooming around like bullets! They obey Newton's Laws. Every time they crash into the wall of the container, they give it a tiny push (momentum change). Billions of crashes per second add up to steady pressure.

Formula: PV = (1/3) N m <v²>

Meaning: * N: Number of molecules * m: Mass per molecule * <v²>: Mean square speed * Pressure is proportional to kinetic energy density.

Q10.5: Speed of Sound & Laplace Correction [3 marks]

Write Newton's formula for the speed of sound in a gas and explain Laplace's correction.

* Newton's Formula: Assumed isothermal propagation: v = √(P/ρ) This gave a value (~280 m/s) much lower than experimental results for air (332 m/s).

Laplace's Correction: Laplace suggested that sound propagation is an adiabatic process (rapid changes, no heat exchange). Formula: v = √(γP/ρ) Where γ = Cp/Cv (adiabatic index). For air, γ ≈ 1.4. This corrected formula matches experimental values accurately.

Q10.6: Organ Pipes [5 marks]

Compare the fundamental frequencies of an open organ pipe and a closed organ pipe of the same length L.

* 1. Open Pipe (Open at both ends): Wavelength λ = 2L Frequency fopen = v / 2L Harmonics: All harmonics present (f, 2f, 3f...)

2. Closed Pipe (Closed at one end): Wavelength λ = 4L Frequency fclosed = v / 4L Harmonics: Only odd harmonics present (f, 3f, 5f...)

Comparison: fopen / fclosed = (v/2L) / (v/4L) = 2 The fundamental frequency of an open pipe is twice that of a closed pipe of the same length.

Q9.6: Mayer's Relation (Cp - Cv) [5 marks]

Derive the relation Cp - Cv = R for an ideal gas.

* Concept: Why are there two specific heats (Cp and Cv)? * Cv (Constant Volume): You heat a box that *can't expand*. All heat goes into raising temperature. (Cheap). * Cp (Constant Pressure): You heat a box that *expands*. The heat has to do TWO jobs: raise temperature AND do work to push the walls out. (Expensive). * So Cp > Cv (specifically, Cp - Cv = R).

Derivation: 1. First Law: ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV 2. At Constant V: ΔW=0 → ΔQv = ΔU = nCvΔT 3. At Constant P: ΔQp = ΔU + PΔV = nCpΔT 4. Since PΔV = nRΔT (Ideal Gas Law): nCpΔT = nCvΔT + nRΔT Cp - Cv = R

Q9.7: Root Mean Square Speed [3 marks]

Calculate the RMS speed of Nitrogen molecules at 300 K. (Molar mass of N₂ = 28 g/mol, R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

* Concept: RMS Speed stands for "Root Mean Square". It's a statistical way to find the "average" speed of gas molecules. * Higher Temperature = Faster Molecules. * Heavier Molecules (higher Mass) = Slower Speed.

Formula: vrms = √(3RT/M)

Calculation: Given: T=300 K, M(N₂)=28e-3 kg/mol.

v = √(3 × 8.314 × 300 / 0.028) v = √(7482.6 / 0.028) = √267235 v = 517 m/s
