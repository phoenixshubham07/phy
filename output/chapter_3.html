A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q3.1: Work by Variable Force [3 marks]

Define work done by a variable force. How is it different from constant force? Explain using a spring as an example.

* Concept: * Constant Force: Pushing a box on smooth floor. The push is same strength throughout. Work is simple multiplication. * Variable Force: Stretching a rubber band. It gets *harder* to pull the more you stretch it. The force "varies" or changes. To calculate work here, we can't just multiply—we assume we pull in tiny steps and add them all up (Integration).

Formula: * Constant: W = F·s * Variable: W = ∫ F(x) dx * Spring Work: W = ∫ kx dx = ½kx²

Key Difference: Constant force implies uniform push/pull. Variable force (like a spring) changes magnitude with position, so average force or calculus is needed.

Q3.2: Work-Energy Theorem [3 marks]

State and derive the work-energy theorem. Explain what it means physically.

* Concept: The Work-Energy Theorem is the bridge between "Action" and "Result". * Action: You do Net Work on an object (push it). * Result: The object speeds up (gains Kinetic Energy). It essentially says: "The work you put in turns into the object's speed energy."

Formula: Wnet = ΔK = Kfinal - Kinitial

Derivation: 1. W = ∫ F dx = ∫ (ma) dx 2. a dx = (v dv/dx) dx = v dv 3. W = m ∫ v dv = m [v²/2] 4. W = ½mv² - ½mu²

Q3.3: Impulse and Momentum [3 marks]

Define impulse and explain how it relates to momentum change. Why do airbags in cars extend the time of impact?

* Concept: Impulse is a large force acting for a very short time (like a bat hitting a ball). It causes a sudden change in momentum (speed). * Airbags: They are soft pillows that make the collision last *longer* ( increase Δt). * Since the total momentum change is fixed (stopping the car), increasing the *time* forces the *impact force* to be smaller. Longer time = Gentler hit.

Formula: * J = Favg × Δt = Δp * Favg = Δp / Δt

Application (Airbags): * Goal: Reduce Force (Favg). * Method: Increase time (Δt) using airbag cushioning. * Since Δp is fixed (impact speed to zero), increasing Δt lowers Favg.

Q3.4: Momentum Conservation in Collisions [3 marks]

State the law of conservation of momentum. Explain why momentum is conserved in collisions but kinetic energy is not conserved in inelastic collisions.

* Concept: Imagine two billiard balls hitting. * Momentum Conservation: Is *always* true. The total "movement" before crash = total "movement" after crash. (Newton's Laws ensure this). * Kinetic Energy: Is fragile. In real crashes (inelastic), some energy is lost to sound ("Clack!"), heat, or denting the ball. So KE is usually *not* conserved.

Formula: pi = pf (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂)

Why KE not conserved in Inelastic: In inelastic collisions, energy transforms into: 1. Heat/Sound. 2. Permanent deformation. 3. So, Kfinal < Kinitial.

Q3.5: Work by Variable Force [5 marks]

A force F = 3x + 2 (in Newtons, where x is in meters) acts on a 2 kg object. Find the work done when the object moves from x = 0 to x = 4 m. Also find the final velocity if it starts from rest.

* Concept: We are given a force `F = 3x + 2` that changes as the object moves (depends on x). 1. Find Work: Since force changes, we integrate (sum up) the force over the distance. 2. Find Speed: Use the Work-Energy Theorem: "Total Work Done = Kinetic Energy Gained".

Formula: * W = ∫ F dx * W = ½mv²

Calculation: (a) Work Done: W = ∫₀⁴ (3x + 2) dx = [1.5x² + 2x]₀⁴ W = 1.5(16) + 2(4) = 24 + 8 = 32 J

(b) Final Velocity: 32 = ½(2)v² v² = 32 v = 5.66 m/s

Q3.6: Momentum Conservation - Perfectly Inelastic Collision [5 marks]

A 3 kg object moving at 8 m/s collides head-on with a 2 kg object moving at 5 m/s in the opposite direction. They stick together after collision. Find: (a) Velocity and direction after collision (b) Loss in kinetic energy (c) Energy converted to other forms

*

Given:

• Object 1: m₁ = 3 kg, u₁ = 8 m/s (let's say rightward, positive)
• Object 2: m₂ = 2 kg, u₂ = -5 m/s (leftward, opposite, negative)

Solution: Concept: "Perfectly Inelastic" implies the objects crash and stick together like wet clay. They move as one single blob afterwards. * Conservation: Momentum is still conserved! Total momentum before = Total momentum of the "blob" after.

Formula: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v

Calculation: (a) Final Velocity: 3(8) + 2(-5) = (3+2)v 24 - 10 = 5v 14 = 5v → v = 2.8 m/s

(b) Energy Loss: Ki = ½(3)(64) + ½(2)(25) = 96 + 25 = 121 J Kf = ½(5)(2.8)² = 19.6 J Loss = 121 - 19.6 = 101.4 J

Q3.7: Elastic Collision [5 marks]

A 4 kg ball moving at 6 m/s elastically collides with a 2 kg ball initially at rest. Find the velocities of both balls after collision.

*

Given:

• m₁ = 4 kg, u₁ = 6 m/s
• m₂ = 2 kg, u₂ = 0

Solution: Concept: "Elastic" implies a bouncy collision (like superballs). * No energy is lost to heat or deformation. * We use two rules: (1) Conservation of Momentum AND (2) Conservation of Kinetic Energy. This lets us solve for the two unknown final speeds.

Formula: * m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ * v₁ = [(m₁-m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂] / (m₁+m₂)

Calculation: m₁=4, u₁=6; m₂=2, u₂=0.

v₁ = [(2)(6)] / 6 = 2 m/s v₂ = [2(4)(6)] / 6 = 8 m/s

Q3.8: Impulse-Momentum Problem [5 marks]

A car of mass 1500 kg moving at 20 m/s hits a wall. The collision lasts 0.1 seconds. The car comes to rest. Find: (a) Change in momentum (b) Average force during collision (c) If with an airbag the collision time extends to 0.5 seconds, what is the new average force? (d) How many times is the force reduced?

*

Given: m = 1500 kg, u = 20 m/s, v = 0, t₁ = 0.1 s, t₂ = 0.5 s

Solution: Concept: This problem compares a "hard crash" vs a "soft crash". * Impulse: The total change in motion (Momentum) is the same in both cases (Car stops). * Force: Force = (Change in Momentum) / (Time taken). * If we make the time 5x longer (using airbag), the crashing force becomes 5x smaller!

Formula: Favg = Δp / Δt

Calculation: (a) Δp: 1500(0 - 20) = -30,000 kgm/s (b) Force (No Airbag): F = 30000 / 0.1 = 300 kN (c) Force (Airbag): F = 30000 / 0.5 = 60 kN (d) Reduction: 300/60 = 5 times

Q3.9: Conservative vs Non-Conservative Forces [3 marks]

Distinguish between conservative and non-conservative forces. Give two examples of each.

* Concept: * Conservative Force (Reliable): Work done depends *only* on start and end points (not the path). Energy is "stored" and can be recovered completely (like gravity or springs). * Non-Conservative Force (Wasteful): Work depends on the path taken. Energy is "lost" to heat/friction and cannot be fully recovered. Long path = more energy lost. Energy lost as heat.

Q3.10: Power Calculation [5 marks]

A machine lifts a 100 kg load to a height of 20 m in 10 seconds. Calculate the power developed by the machine. (g = 10 m/s²)

* Concept: Power is the rate of doing work.

Formula: P = W/t = mgh/t

Calculation: W = 100 × 10 × 20 = 20,000 J P = 20,000 / 10 P = 2000 W = 2 kW
