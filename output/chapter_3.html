A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q3.1: Work by Variable Force [3 marks]

Define work done by a variable force. How is it different from constant force? Explain using a spring as an example.

* Constant Force: W = F·s (Simple product). Variable Force: W = ∫ F(x) dx (Integration required).

Spring Example: Force F = -kx (Variable, depends on x). Work to stretch: W = ∫₀ˣ kx dx = k[x²/2] = ½kx²

Physical Differences: 1. Graphical Interpretation:

• Constant force: W = F × s (rectangle under F-s graph)
• Variable force: W = Area under F-s curve

Q3.2: Work-Energy Theorem [3 marks]

State and derive the work-energy theorem. Explain what it means physically.

* Statement: Total Work Done = Change in Kinetic Energy Wnet = ΔK = Kfinal - Kinitial

Derivation (1D): W = ∫ F dx = ∫ (ma) dx W = m ∫ (v dv/dx) dx = m ∫ v dv W = m [v²/2] from u to v W = ½mv² - ½mu²

Physical Meaning: 1. Positive Work: W > 0 → ΔKE > 0 → Object speeds up 2. Negative Work: W < 0 → ΔKE < 0 → Object slows down 3. Zero Work: W = 0 → ΔKE = 0 → Constant speed

Q3.3: Impulse and Momentum [3 marks]

Define impulse and explain how it relates to momentum change. Why do airbags in cars extend the time of impact?

* Momentum (p): p = mv (mass × velocity). Vector quantity. Impulse (J): J = Favg × Δt (average force × time interval). Vector quantity.

Impulse-Momentum Theorem: Impulse equals the change in momentum: J = Δp = pfinal - pinitial

Airbags and Impact Time:

• From J = Favg × Δt, we have Favg = Δp / Δt.
• Airbags increase the collision time (Δt) for the occupant.
• Since Δp (change in momentum) is fixed (from initial speed to zero), increasing Δt reduces the average force (Favg) experienced by the occupant.
• This prevents severe injury by spreading the force over a longer period.

Q3.4: Momentum Conservation in Collisions [3 marks]

State the law of conservation of momentum. Explain why momentum is conserved in collisions but kinetic energy is not conserved in inelastic collisions.

* Law of Conservation of Momentum: In an isolated system (no external forces), the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after the collision. pbefore = pafter (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂)

Why Momentum is Conserved:

• Collision forces are internal forces (Newton's 3rd Law: F₁₂ = -F₂₁).
• The impulse on one object is equal and opposite to the impulse on the other, leading to equal and opposite changes in momentum.
• Thus, the total momentum of the system remains constant.

Why Kinetic Energy is NOT Conserved in Inelastic Collisions:

KEafter < KEbefore (energy "lost" to heat, sound, deformation)

Energy "Loss" Calculation: ΔKE = KEafter - KEbefore < 0 (negative means energy lost)

This "lost" energy becomes internal energy, heat, etc.

Key Point: Momentum is ALWAYS conserved (vector law), but mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved only in elastic processes. Inelastic processes dissipate mechanical energy.

Q3.5: Work by Variable Force [5 marks]

A force F = 3x + 2 (in Newtons, where x is in meters) acts on a 2 kg object. Find the work done when the object moves from x = 0 to x = 4 m. Also find the final velocity if it starts from rest.

*

Given: F(x) = 3x + 2, m = 2 kg, initial position = 0, final position = 4 m, u = 0

(a) Work Done: W = ∫₀⁴ F(x) dx = ∫₀⁴ (3x + 2) dx

W = [3x²/2 + 2x]₀⁴

W = [3(4)²/2 + 2(4)] - [0]

W = [3(16)/2 + 8] = [24 + 8]

W = 32 Joules

(b) Final Velocity: Using work-energy theorem: W = ΔKE = ½mv² - ½mu²

32 = ½(2)v² - 0

32 = v²

v = √32 = 4√2 = 5.66 m/s

Verification using integration: Using F = ma = m(dv/dt): 3x + 2 = 2(dv/dt)

Using dv/dt = v(dv/dx): 3x + 2 = 2v(dv/dx)

(3x + 2)dx = 2v dv

Integrating both sides: ∫₀⁴ (3x + 2)dx = ∫₀ᵥ 2v dv

32 = [v²]₀ᵥ = v² ✓

Q3.6: Momentum Conservation - Perfectly Inelastic Collision [5 marks]

A 3 kg object moving at 8 m/s collides head-on with a 2 kg object moving at 5 m/s in the opposite direction. They stick together after collision. Find: (a) Velocity and direction after collision (b) Loss in kinetic energy (c) Energy converted to other forms

*

Given:

• Object 1: m₁ = 3 kg, u₁ = 8 m/s (let's say rightward, positive)
• Object 2: m₂ = 2 kg, u₂ = -5 m/s (leftward, opposite, negative)

(a) Velocity After Collision:

Using conservation of momentum: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v

3(8) + 2(-5) = (3 + 2)v

24 - 10 = 5v

14 = 5v

v = 2.8 m/s (rightward, positive direction)

(b) Kinetic Energy Before and After:

KEbefore = ½m₁u₁² + ½m₂u₂² = ½(3)(8)² + ½(2)(5)² = ½(3)(64) + ½(2)(25) = 96 + 25 = 121 Joules

KEafter = ½(m₁ + m₂)v² = ½(5)(2.8)² = ½(5)(7.84) = 19.6 Joules

(c) Loss in Kinetic Energy:

ΔKE = KEafter - KEbefore = 19.6 - 121 = -101.4 Joules

Energy "lost" (converted to other forms) = 101.4 J

This energy becomes:

• Heat (warming objects)
• Sound (collision noise)
• Deformation energy
• Internal energy

Percentage of energy lost: = (101.4 / 121) × 100% = 83.8%

Only 16.2% of initial KE remains in the moving combined mass.

Q3.7: Elastic Collision [5 marks]

A 4 kg ball moving at 6 m/s elastically collides with a 2 kg ball initially at rest. Find the velocities of both balls after collision.

*

Given:

• m₁ = 4 kg, u₁ = 6 m/s
• m₂ = 2 kg, u₂ = 0

For Elastic Collision, we use two equations:

1) Conservation of Momentum: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ 4(6) + 2(0) = 4v₁ + 2v₂ 24 = 4v₁ + 2v₂ ... (1)

2) Conservation of KE: ½m₁u₁² + ½m₂u₂² = ½m₁v₁² + ½m₂v₂² ½(4)(36) + 0 = ½(4)v₁² + ½(2)v₂² 72 = 2v₁² + v₂² ... (2)

Using Formulas for Elastic Collision:

v₁ = ((m₁ - m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂) / (m₁ + m₂) v₁ = ((4 - 2)(6) + 0) / (4 + 2) v₁ = (2 × 6) / 6 v₁ = 2 m/s

v₂ = ((m₂ - m₁)u₂ + 2m₁u₁) / (m₁ + m₂) v₂ = ((2 - 4)(0) + 2(4)(6)) / 6 v₂ = (0 + 48) / 6 v₂ = 8 m/s

Verification: Momentum before: 4(6) + 2(0) = 24 kg·m/s Momentum after: 4(2) + 2(8) = 8 + 16 = 24 kg·m/s ✓

KE before: ½(4)(36) + 0 = 72 J KE after: ½(4)(4) + ½(2)(64) = 8 + 64 = 72 J ✓

Physical Interpretation:

• Ball 1 slows from 6 m/s to 2 m/s (hits ball 2)
• Ball 2 accelerates from 0 to 8 m/s (being struck)
• Ball 2 moves faster than ball 1 after collision (characteristic of elastic collision with lighter object)

Q3.8: Impulse-Momentum Problem [5 marks]

A car of mass 1500 kg moving at 20 m/s hits a wall. The collision lasts 0.1 seconds. The car comes to rest. Find: (a) Change in momentum (b) Average force during collision (c) If with an airbag the collision time extends to 0.5 seconds, what is the new average force? (d) How many times is the force reduced?

*

Given: m = 1500 kg, u = 20 m/s, v = 0, t₁ = 0.1 s, t₂ = 0.5 s

(a) Change in Momentum: Δp = m(v - u) = 1500(0 - 20) = -30,000 kg·m/s

The negative sign indicates the momentum change is opposite to initial motion. Magnitude: |Δp| = 30,000 kg·m/s

(b) Average Force Without Airbag: Using impulse-momentum theorem: Favg = |Δp| / t₁ = 30,000 / 0.1 = 300,000 N = 300 kN

This is an enormous force! (equivalent to weight of 30,000 kg or 30 tons!)

(c) Average Force With Airbag: Favg' = |Δp| / t₂ = 30,000 / 0.5 = 60,000 N = 60 kN

(d) Force Reduction Factor: Ratio = Favg / Favg' = 300,000 / 60,000 = 5 times reduction

Or: Ratio = t₂ / t₁ = 0.5 / 0.1 = 5

Physical Implications:

• Without airbag: 300 kN can cause severe injuries, crushing organs
• With airbag: 60 kN spread over body, survivable force level
• By extending collision time 5×, force reduced by 5×
• Typical airbag force threshold for survival: 40-80 kN
• Therefore, airbag keeps force within survivable range

This demonstrates why airbags are crucial safety devices!

Q3.9: Conservative vs Non-Conservative Forces [3 marks]

Distinguish between conservative and non-conservative forces. Give two examples of each.

* Conservative: Work depends only on End Points. Loop Work = 0. * Examples: Gravity, Spring Force.

Non-Conservative: Work depends on Path. Energy lost as heat. * Examples: Friction, Air Drag.

Q3.10: Power Calculation [5 marks]

A machine lifts a 100 kg load to a height of 20 m in 10 seconds. Calculate the power developed by the machine. (g = 10 m/s²)

* Given: m=100 kg, h=20 m, t=10 s.

Power (P) = Work / Time W = mgh = 100 × 10 × 20 = 20,000 J P = 20,000 / 10 P = 2,000 W = 2 kW
