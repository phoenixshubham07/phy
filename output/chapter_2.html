A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q2.1: Principle of Independence of Motion [3 marks]

Explain the principle of independence of motion in projectile motion. Why can horizontal and vertical motions be analyzed separately?

*

> Principle:

> The horizontal and vertical components of projectile motion are completely independent of each other. Motion along one axis has zero effect on motion along the perpendicular axis.

Axis Analysis Table:

AxisForceAccelerationMotion TypeEquation
Horizontal (X)None (0)\(ax = 0\)Uniform Velocity\(x = ux t\)
Vertical (Y)Gravity (\(mg\))\(ay = -g\)Uniform Acceleration\(y = uy t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2\)

Reasoning:

  • Forces are vectors.
  • Gravity acts purely vertically (down). It has no component horizontally (\(\cos 90^\circ = 0\)).
  • Therefore, the horizontal speed never changes (ignoring air), while the vertical speed changes constantly.

Q2.2: Maximum Height and Time of Flight [3 marks]

Derive the expressions for maximum height and time of flight of a projectile launched at angle θ with initial velocity u.

*

> Definitions:

> * Max Height (\(H\)): Vertical distance where \(vy = 0\).

> * Time of Flight (\(T\)): Total duration the projectile remains in air (\(y_{final} = 0\)).

Derivation Steps:

ParameterCondition UsedEquation Derived
Max Height (\(H\))At peak, vertical velocity \(vy = 0\)
\[ H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g} \]
Time of Flight (\(T\))Total vertical displacement \(y = 0\)
\[ T = \frac{2u \sin \theta}{g} \]

Detailed Proof:

1. For H: Use \(v^2 - u^2 = 2as\).

  • \(0^2 - (u \sin \theta)^2 = 2(-g)H \implies H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}\)

2. For T: Use \(y = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\).

  • \(0 = (u \sin \theta)T - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 \implies T = \frac{2u \sin \theta}{g}\)

Q2.3: Range of Projectile [3 marks]

Show that the range of a projectile is maximum at 45° and why complementary angles (θ and 90°-θ) give the same range.

*

> Definition (Range):

> Horizontal distance covered during the time of flight. Determined by the product of horizontal velocity and total time.

Formula:

\[ R = \frac{u^2 \sin 2\theta}{g} \]

Logic Table:

CaseAngleMathematical ReasonResult
Max Range\(\theta = 45^\circ\)\(\sin 2\theta\) reaches max value (1) at \(90^\circ\) (\(2\times45\)).\(R_{max} = u^2/g\)
Complementary\(90^\circ - \theta\)\(\sin(2(90-\theta)) = \sin(180-2\theta) = \sin 2\theta\)Ranges are Equal

Interpretation:

  • 45°: The perfect compromise between "Going High" (Time) and "Going Fast Forward" (Speed).
  • Complementary: A high lob (\(60^\circ\)) and a low drive (\(30^\circ\)) land at the same spot, though their flight times differ.

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q2.4: Basic Projectile Calculation [5 marks]

A projectile is fired horizontally with initial velocity 40 m/s from the top of a cliff 80 m high. Find:

(a) Time of flight

(b) Horizontal range

(c) Velocity when it hits the ground

(d) Angle of velocity at impact

*

Concept:

Horizontal Projectile Motion: Launch angle is 0°.

  • Initial Velocity: \(ux = 40, uy = 0\).
  • Motion acts like "Free Fall" + "Constant Drift".

Input Data Table:

VariableValue
Initial Velocity (\(ux\))40 m/s
Height (\(h\))80 m
Gravity (\(g\))10 m/s²
Initial Vertical (\(uy\))0 m/s

Calculations:

(a) Time of Flight (\(t\)):

Using \(h = \frac{1}{2}gt^2\):

\[ 80 = \frac{1}{2}(10)t^2 \implies 16 = t^2 \implies \mathbf{t = 4 \text{ s}} \]

(b) Horizontal Range (\(R\)):

\[ R = ux \times t = 40 \times 4 = \mathbf{160 \text{ m}} \]

(c) Final Velocity (\(v\)):

  • \(vx = 40\) m/s (Constant)
  • \(vy = gt = 10(4) = 40\) m/s
  • Resultant: \(v = \sqrt{40^2 + 40^2} = \mathbf{56.57 \text{ m/s}} \)

(d) Angle (\(\alpha\)):

\[ \tan \alpha = \frac{vy}{vx} = \frac{40}{40} = 1 \implies \mathbf{\alpha = 45^\circ} \]

Q2.5: Projectile at an Angle [5 marks]

A projectile is launched at 30° to the horizontal with initial speed 50 m/s. Find:

(a) Maximum height reached

(b) Time to reach maximum height

(c) Horizontal range

(d) Time of flight

(Use g = 10 m/s²)

*

Concept:

This is a standard "cannonball" problem. We take the launch velocity (50 m/s) and split it into two parts:

  • Upward part (u sinθ): Fights gravity, decides height and time.
  • Forward part (u cosθ): Drives the distance (range).

Formula:

  • H = (u² sin²θ) / 2g
  • R = (u² sin2θ) / g
  • T = (2u sinθ) / g

Calculation:

Given: u=50, θ=30°, g=10.

(a) Max Height:

H = (50² × 0.5²) / 20 = 625/20 = 31.25 m

(b) Time to Max Height:

t = (u sinθ)/g = (50×0.5)/10 = 2.5 s

(c) Range:

R = (2500 × sin60°)/10 = 250(0.866) = 216.5 m

(d) Total Time:

T = 2 × 2.5 = 5 s

Q2.6: Projectile - Height and Range Problem [5 marks]

A ball is projected at angle 37° to the horizontal with speed 20 m/s. A wall of height 5 m stands at horizontal distance 30 m. Will the ball clear the wall? If yes, by how much? (sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8, g = 10 m/s²)

*

Concept:

We need to find the height (y) of the ball exactly when it is 30m away (x=30).

Think of the path as a curve (parabola). We use the Equation of Trajectory which gives us the height 'y' for any horizontal distance 'x'. If at x=30, the ball's height y is more than 5m, it clears the wall!

Formula:

Trajectory Equation: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)

Calculation:

x = 30, θ = 37°, u = 20.

y = 30(0.75) - [10(900)] / [2(400)(0.64)]

y = 22.5 - 9000/512

y = 22.5 - 17.58

y = 4.92 m

Conclusion:

Wall height = 5 m. Projectile height = 4.92 m.

Ball falls short by 0.08 m (Hits the wall).

Q2.7: Two-Stage Projectile Motion [5 marks]

A cricketer throws a ball at 35° to the horizontal with initial speed 25 m/s. Another cricketer standing at horizontal distance 60 m wants to catch it at the same height from which it was thrown. At what height should he position his hands if he can catch a ball at heights from ground level up to 2.2 m?

*

Concept:

The catch is that the second cricketer is standing 60m away, but we calculate the ball's natural range first. If the ball naturally lands at 58m, it will never reach the guy at 60m in the air! It will bounce on the ground first. So the "catch height" is effectively ground level.

Formula:

y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)

Calculation:

x = 60 m, u = 25 m/s, θ = 35°.

Actual Range R = (625 × 0.94)/10 = 58.75 m.

Since target distance 60 m > Range 58.75 m, the ball hits the ground before reaching the fielder.

Height at x=60 m is effectively 0 (Ground level).

Conclusion:

Fielder can catch it at ground level (0 m) if he moves slightly forward to 58.75 m.

Q2.8: Trajectory Equation Derivation [5 marks]

Derive the equation of the trajectory of a projectile: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ). Prove that the path is parabolic.

*

Concept:

We usually have two equations: one for x (distance) involving time 't', and one for y (height) involving time 't'.

To see the shape of the path, we need to remove 't' from the picture. By plugging the time from x-equation into the y-equation, we get a single formula linking Height (y) directly to Distance (x). This formula looks like `y = ax - bx²`, which is the mathematical shape of a Parabola.

Formula:

  • x = (u cosθ)t
  • y = (u sinθ)t - ½gt²

Derivation:

1. From x-eqn: t = x / (u cosθ)

2. Substitute into y-eqn:

y = (u sinθ)[x / (u cosθ)] - ½g[x / (u cosθ)]²

y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ)

Conclusion:

This calculates a parabola (\(y = ax - bx^2\)).
