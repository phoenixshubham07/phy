A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q2.1: Principle of Independence of Motion [3 marks]

Explain the principle of independence of motion in projectile motion. Why can horizontal and vertical motions be analyzed separately?

* Principle: Horizontal and Vertical motions are independent. * Horizontal: No acceleration (ax = 0). Velocity is constant. * Vertical: Constant acceleration due to gravity (ay = -g).

Why separate? Gravity acts only vertically. It does not affect horizontal speed. We can solve x and y equations separately and combine them via time 't'.

---

Q2.2: Maximum Height and Time of Flight [3 marks]

Derive the expressions for maximum height and time of flight of a projectile launched at angle θ with initial velocity u.

* 1. Max Height (H): At top, vertical velocity vy = 0. Using v² - u² = 2as: 0 - (u sinθ)² = 2(-g)H H = (u² sin²θ) / 2g

2. Time of Flight (T): Total vertical displacement = 0. Using s = ut + ½at²: 0 = (u sinθ)T - ½gT² T(u sinθ - ½gT) = 0 T = (2u sinθ) / g

---

Q2.3: Range of Projectile [3 marks]

Show that the range of a projectile is maximum at 45° and why complementary angles (θ and 90°-θ) give the same range.

* Range Formula: R = (u² sin2θ) / g

Max Range: * R is max when sin2θ = 1. * 2θ = 90° → θ = 45°.

Complementary Angles: * For angle θ: R = u² sin(2θ) / g * For angle (90-θ): R = u² sin(180-2θ) / g * Since sin(180-x) = sin(x), Ranges are equal.

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q2.4: Basic Projectile Calculation [5 marks]

A projectile is fired horizontally with initial velocity 40 m/s from the top of a cliff 80 m high. Find: (a) Time of flight (b) Horizontal range (c) Velocity when it hits the ground (d) Angle of velocity at impact

*

Given: ux = 40 m/s (horizontal), uy = 0 (fired horizontally), h = 80 m

(a) Time of Flight: Using h = ½gt² 80 = ½ × 10 × t² 80 = 5t² t² = 16 t = 4 seconds

(b) Horizontal Range: R = ux × t = 40 × 4 = 160 m

(c) Final Velocity: Horizontal component (constant): vx = 40 m/s Vertical component: vy = uy + gt = 0 + 10 × 4 = 40 m/s

Magnitude: v = √(vx² + vy²) = √(40² + 40²) = √3200 = 40√2 = 56.57 m/s

(d) Angle at Impact: tan(α) = vy / vx = 40/40 = 1 α = 45° below horizontal

Q2.5: Projectile at an Angle [5 marks]

A projectile is launched at 30° to the horizontal with initial speed 50 m/s. Find: (a) Maximum height reached (b) Time to reach maximum height (c) Horizontal range (d) Time of flight (Use g = 10 m/s²)

*

Given: θ = 30°, u = 50 m/s, g = 10 m/s²

Initial Components:

• ux = u cosθ = 50 cos(30°) = 50 × 0.866 = 43.3 m/s
• uy = u sinθ = 50 sin(30°) = 50 × 0.5 = 25 m/s

(a) Maximum Height: H = (uy²) / (2g) = (25²) / (2 × 10) = 625 / 20 = 31.25 m

Or: H = (u² sin²θ) / (2g) = (50² × sin²(30°)) / 20 = (2500 × 0.25) / 20 = 31.25 m ✓

(b) Time to Maximum Height: tmax = uy / g = 25 / 10 = 2.5 seconds

(c) Horizontal Range: R = (u² sin2θ) / g = (50² × sin(60°)) / 10 = (2500 × 0.866) / 10 = 216.5 m

Or: R = ux × T = 43.3 × 5 = 216.5 m ✓

(d) Time of Flight: T = (2u sinθ) / g = (2 × 50 × sin(30°)) / 10 = (100 × 0.5) / 10 = 5 seconds

Q2.6: Projectile - Height and Range Problem [5 marks]

A ball is projected at angle 37° to the horizontal with speed 20 m/s. A wall of height 5 m stands at horizontal distance 30 m. Will the ball clear the wall? If yes, by how much? (sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8, g = 10 m/s²)

*

Given: θ = 37°, u = 20 m/s, wall height = 5 m, wall distance = 30 m

Initial Components:

• ux = 20 × 0.8 = 16 m/s
• uy = 20 × 0.6 = 12 m/s

Time to reach wall (x = 30 m): x = ux × t 30 = 16 × t t = 1.875 s

Height at wall: y = uy × t - ½gt² y = 12 × 1.875 - ½ × 10 × (1.875)² y = 22.5 - 5 × 3.516 y = 22.5 - 17.58 y = 4.92 m

Analysis: Wall height = 5 m Ball height at wall = 4.92 m

The ball reaches only 4.92 m, while the wall is 5 m high. The ball does NOT clear the wall. It falls short by 5 - 4.92 = 0.08 m = 8 cm

Alternatively, if we check the trajectory equation: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ) y = 30 × tan(37°) - (10 × 30²)/(2 × 20² × 0.8²) y = 30 × 0.75 - 9000/(2 × 400 × 0.64) y = 22.5 - 9000/512 = 22.5 - 17.58 = 4.92 m ✓

Q2.7: Two-Stage Projectile Motion [5 marks]

A cricketer throws a ball at 35° to the horizontal with initial speed 25 m/s. Another cricketer standing at horizontal distance 60 m wants to catch it at the same height from which it was thrown. At what height should he position his hands if he can catch a ball at heights from ground level up to 2.2 m?

*

Given: θ = 35°, u = 25 m/s, R = 60 m, g = 10 m/s² sin35° = 0.574, cos35° = 0.819, tan35° = 0.700

Method 1: Using Range Formula First Actual range for this projectile: Ractual = (u² sin2θ) / g = (25² × sin(70°)) / 10 = (625 × 0.94) / 10 = 58.75 m

Time to reach horizontal distance of 60 m: t = 60 / (u cosθ) = 60 / (25 × 0.819) = 60 / 20.475 = 2.93 s

Height at this point: y = u sinθ × t - ½gt² y = 25 × 0.574 × 2.93 - ½ × 10 × (2.93)² y = 14.35 × 2.93 - 5 × 8.585 y = 42.05 - 42.925 y = -0.875 m (below launch height)

Physical Interpretation: The ball has passed its landing point and is on the way down. It would have landed at ground level at approximately: Range = 58.75 m (slightly before 60 m)

At 60 m distance (beyond normal range), the ball is descending.

The catcher cannot catch the ball at 60 m since it lands before reaching there. If the problem meant he can run back to catch at the landing point: The ball lands at 58.75 m horizontally at ground level (0 m height).

The catcher's catching height range is 0 to 2.2 m. Since the ball lands at exactly ground level, yes, he can catch it (barely) at height 0 m.

Q2.8: Trajectory Equation Derivation [5 marks]

Derive the equation of the trajectory of a projectile: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ). Prove that the path is parabolic.

* Derivation: 1. x = (u cosθ)t → t = x / (u cosθ) 2. y = (u sinθ)t - ½gt²

Substitute t: y = (u sinθ)(x / u cosθ) - ½g(x / u cosθ)² y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ)

Conclusion: Equation is quadratic in x ($y = ax - bx^2$). Hence, the path is a parabola.
