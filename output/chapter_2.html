A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q2.1: Principle of Independence of Motion [3 marks]

Explain the principle of independence of motion in projectile motion. Why can horizontal and vertical motions be analyzed separately?

* Concept: The Principle of Independence states that perpendicular motion components do not affect each other. Horizontal motion is uniform (constant velocity), while vertical motion is accelerated (gravity).

Formula: * x-axis: ax = 0 → vx = constant * y-axis: ay = -g → vy changes

Key Reasoning: Gravity acts only vertically. There is no force in the horizontal direction (neglecting air resistance). Thus, horizontal and vertical motions can be analyzed using separate independent equations connected only by time 't'.

Q2.2: Maximum Height and Time of Flight [3 marks]

Derive the expressions for maximum height and time of flight of a projectile launched at angle θ with initial velocity u.

* Concept: Max Height H is reached when vertical velocity becomes zero. Time of Flight T is the total time to return to launch level.

Formula: * v² - u² = 2as * s = ut + ½at²

Derivation: 1. Max Height (H): At top, vy = 0. 0² - (u sinθ)² = 2(-g)H H = (u² sin²θ) / 2g

2. Time of Flight (T): Total vertical displacement y = 0. 0 = (u sinθ)T - ½gT² T = (2u sinθ) / g

Q2.3: Range of Projectile [3 marks]

Show that the range of a projectile is maximum at 45° and why complementary angles (θ and 90°-θ) give the same range.

* Concept: Range is the horizontal distance covered. It depends on the double angle sine function.

Formula: R = (u² sin2θ) / g

Explanation: 1. Maximum Range: R is max when sin2θ = 1 → 2θ = 90° → θ = 45°.

2. Complementary Angles: For angles θ and (90°-θ): * sin(2θ) * sin(2(90°-θ)) = sin(180°-2θ) = sin(2θ) * Result: R₁ = R₂ (Ranges are identical).

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q2.4: Basic Projectile Calculation [5 marks]

A projectile is fired horizontally with initial velocity 40 m/s from the top of a cliff 80 m high. Find: (a) Time of flight (b) Horizontal range (c) Velocity when it hits the ground (d) Angle of velocity at impact

* Concept: Horizontal projectile motion has constant horizontal velocity and free-fall vertical motion.

Formula: * h = ½gt² * R = ux × t * v = √(vx² + vy²)

Calculation: (a) Time of Flight: 80 = ½(10)t² → t²=16 t = 4 s

(b) Range: R = 40 × 4 = 160 m

(c) Final Velocity: vx = 40 m/s vy = gt = 10(4) = 40 m/s v = √(40² + 40²) = 56.57 m/s

(d) Angle: tan α = 40/40 = 1 → α = 45°

Q2.5: Projectile at an Angle [5 marks]

A projectile is launched at 30° to the horizontal with initial speed 50 m/s. Find: (a) Maximum height reached (b) Time to reach maximum height (c) Horizontal range (d) Time of flight (Use g = 10 m/s²)

* Concept: Standard projectile motion equations apply directly.

Formula: * H = (u² sin²θ) / 2g * R = (u² sin2θ) / g * T = (2u sinθ) / g

Calculation: Given: u=50, θ=30°, g=10.

(a) Max Height: H = (50² × 0.5²) / 20 = 625/20 = 31.25 m

(b) Time to Max Height: t = (u sinθ)/g = (50×0.5)/10 = 2.5 s

(c) Range: R = (2500 × sin60°)/10 = 250(0.866) = 216.5 m

(d) Total Time: T = 2 × 2.5 = 5 s

Q2.6: Projectile - Height and Range Problem [5 marks]

A ball is projected at angle 37° to the horizontal with speed 20 m/s. A wall of height 5 m stands at horizontal distance 30 m. Will the ball clear the wall? If yes, by how much? (sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8, g = 10 m/s²)

* Concept: We need to check the height of the projectile at a specific horizontal distance.

Formula: Trajectory Equation: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)

Calculation: x = 30, θ = 37°, u = 20.

y = 30(0.75) - [10(900)] / [2(400)(0.64)] y = 22.5 - 9000/512 y = 22.5 - 17.58 y = 4.92 m

Conclusion: Wall height = 5 m. Projectile height = 4.92 m. Ball falls short by 0.08 m (Hits the wall).

Q2.7: Two-Stage Projectile Motion [5 marks]

A cricketer throws a ball at 35° to the horizontal with initial speed 25 m/s. Another cricketer standing at horizontal distance 60 m wants to catch it at the same height from which it was thrown. At what height should he position his hands if he can catch a ball at heights from ground level up to 2.2 m?

* Concept: Calculate vertical displacement at a horizontal distance R > Range.

Formula: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)

Calculation: x = 60 m, u = 25 m/s, θ = 35°. Actual Range R = (625 × 0.94)/10 = 58.75 m.

Since target distance 60 m > Range 58.75 m, the ball hits the ground before reaching the fielder. Height at x=60 m is effectively 0 (Ground level).

Conclusion: Fielder can catch it at ground level (0 m) if he moves slightly forward to 58.75 m.

Q2.8: Trajectory Equation Derivation [5 marks]

Derive the equation of the trajectory of a projectile: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ). Prove that the path is parabolic.

* Concept: Eliminate time 't' from the horizontal and vertical equations to get y as a function of x.

Formula: * x = (u cosθ)t * y = (u sinθ)t - ½gt²

Derivation: 1. From x-eqn: t = x / (u cosθ) 2. Substitute into y-eqn: y = (u sinθ)[x / (u cosθ)] - ½g[x / (u cosθ)]² y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ)

Conclusion: This calculates a parabola ($y = ax - bx^2$).
