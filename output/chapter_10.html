A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q10.1: Standing Waves on a String [3 marks]

Explain how standing waves are formed on a stretched string. Define nodes and antinodes. What is the formula for the speed of a transverse wave on a string?

* Formation of Standing Waves: Standing waves are formed when two identical waves traveling in opposite directions superpose (interfere). On a stretched string fixed at both ends, the incident wave reflects from the fixed ends and interferes with the incoming wave.

Nodes and Antinodes:

• Nodes: Points of zero displacement (amplitude = 0). They occur where destructive interference is complete. At fixed ends, nodes always form.
• Antinodes: Points of maximum displacement (amplitude = max). They occur where constructive interference is complete.

Wave Speed Formula: The speed of a transverse wave on a stretched string is given by: v = √(T/μ)

Where:

• v = wave speed (m/s)
• T = tension in the string (N)
• μ = linear mass density (mass per unit length, kg/m)

Harmonics: The string vibrates in natural modes called harmonics. Length L = n(λ/2), where n = 1, 2, 3... Frequency fn = n(v/2L)

Q10.2: Doppler Effect [3 marks]

Define the Doppler effect. Giving the general formula for apparent frequency, explain how frequency changes when a source approaches an observer.

* Definition: The Doppler effect is the apparent change in frequency of a wave detected by an observer because of relative motion between the source of the wave and the observer.

General Formula: f' = f [(v ± vo) / (v ∓ vs)]

Where:

• f' = apparent frequency
• f = actual source frequency
• v = speed of sound in the medium
• vo = speed of observer
• vs = speed of source

Sign Convention (Source Approaching Observer):

• Observer is stationary (vo = 0)
• Source moves towards observer (vs is positive in direction of sound, but effectively reduces wavelength)
• Formula becomes: f' = f [v / (v - vs)]
• Since denominator (v - vs) is smaller than v, the ratio is > 1.
• Therefore, f' > f (Frequency increases).
• Pitch appears higher.

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q10.3: String Tension and Frequency [5 marks]

A guitar string of length 0.8 m and mass 4 grams is under a tension of 500 N. (a) Calculate the speed of the wave on the string. (b) Find the fundamental frequency (1st harmonic). (c) Find the frequency of the 3rd harmonic.

*

Given:

• L = 0.8 m
• m = 4 g = 0.004 kg
• T = 500 N

Linear Mass Density (μ): μ = m/L = 0.004 / 0.8 = 0.005 kg/m

(a) Wave Speed: v = √(T/μ) v = √(500 / 0.005) v = √(100,000) v = 316.2 m/s

(b) Fundamental Frequency (n=1): For fundamental mode, L = λ/2 → λ = 2L f₁ = v / λ = v / 2L f₁ = 316.2 / (2 × 0.8) f₁ = 316.2 / 1.6 f₁ = 197.6 Hz

(c) Third Harmonic (n=3): f₃ = 3 × f₁ f₃ = 3 × 197.6 f₃ = 592.8 Hz

(This produces a higher pitch note, an overtone)

Q10.4: Doppler Effect Calculation [5 marks]

A train blowing its whistle at frequency 400 Hz approaches a stationary observer on the platform at a speed of 36 km/h. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s. (a) What is the apparent frequency heard by the observer as the train approaches? (b) What is the apparent frequency heard after the train passes the observer? (c) What is the change in frequency?

*

Given:

• f = 400 Hz
• vs = 36 km/h = 36 × (5/18) = 10 m/s
• vo = 0 (observer stationary)
• v = 340 m/s

(a) Train Approaching: Formula: f' = f [v / (v - vs)] fapproach = 400 [340 / (340 - 10)] fapproach = 400 [340 / 330] fapproach = 400 × 1.0303 fapproach = 412.1 Hz

(b) Train Receding (Moving Away): Formula: f'' = f [v / (v + vs)] frecede = 400 [340 / (340 + 10)] frecede = 400 [340 / 350] frecede = 400 × 0.9714 frecede = 388.6 Hz

(c) Change in Frequency: Δf = fapproach - frecede Δf = 412.1 - 388.6 Δf = 23.5 Hz

(This sudden drop in pitch is characteristic of the Doppler effect on passing)

Q10.5: Speed of Sound & Laplace Correction [3 marks]

Write Newton's formula for the speed of sound in a gas and explain Laplace's correction.

* Newton's Formula: Assumed isothermal propagation: v = √(P/ρ) This gave a value (~280 m/s) much lower than experimental results for air (332 m/s).

Laplace's Correction: Laplace suggested that sound propagation is an adiabatic process (rapid changes, no heat exchange). Formula: v = √(γP/ρ) Where γ = Cp/Cv (adiabatic index). For air, γ ≈ 1.4. This corrected formula matches experimental values accurately.

Q10.6: Organ Pipes [5 marks]

Compare the fundamental frequencies of an open organ pipe and a closed organ pipe of the same length L.

* 1. Open Pipe (Open at both ends): Wavelength λ = 2L Frequency fopen = v / 2L Harmonics: All harmonics present (f, 2f, 3f...)

2. Closed Pipe (Closed at one end): Wavelength λ = 4L Frequency fclosed = v / 4L Harmonics: Only odd harmonics present (f, 3f, 5f...)

Comparison: fopen / fclosed = (v/2L) / (v/4L) = 2 The fundamental frequency of an open pipe is twice that of a closed pipe of the same length.
