Q10.1: Standing Waves on a String [3 marks]

Explain how standing waves are formed on a stretched string. Define nodes and antinodes. What is the formula for the speed of a transverse wave on a string?

*

> Definition:

> Standing Waves result from the superposition of two waves of identical frequency relative to opposite directions. They do not transmit energy; they oscillate in place.

Wave Anatomy:

PartFull NameMotionDisplacement
NodeNStationaryZero
AntinodeAOscillatingMaximum

Nodes at Ends (x=0, L), Antinode in middle.

Formula:

v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}

(T = Tension, \mu = Mass per unit length)

Q10.2: Doppler Effect [3 marks]

Define the Doppler effect. Giving the general formula for apparent frequency, explain how frequency changes when a source approaches an observer.

*

> Definition:

> The apparent change in frequency of a wave due to relative motion between the source and the observer.

Effect Summary:

ActionFrequency ChangePitch Perception
ApproachingIncreases (\uparrow)Higher ("Eeee")
RecedingDecreases (\downarrow)Lower ("Oooo")

Formula:

f' = f \left( \frac{v \pm vo}{v \mp vs} \right)

Analogy:

A car approaching you crunches the sound waves together (shorter wavelength = higher freq). A car leaving stretches them out.

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q10.3: String Tension and Frequency [5 marks]

A guitar string of length 0.8 m and mass 4 grams is under a tension of 500 N.

(a) Calculate the speed of the wave on the string.

(b) Find the fundamental frequency (1st harmonic).

(c) Find the frequency of the 3rd harmonic.

*

Concept:

The "Pitch" (Frequency) of a string depends on 3 things:

1. Length: Short string = High pitch.

2. Tension: Tight string = High pitch.

3. Thickness (Mass): Heavy string = Low pitch.

We calculate wave speed first, then frequency.

Formula:

  • μ = m/L
  • v = √(T/μ)
  • fn = n(v/2L)

Calculation:

(a) Wave Speed:

μ = 0.004 / 0.8 = 0.005 kg/m.

v = √(500/0.005) = √100,000 = 316.2 m/s

(b) Fund. Freq (n=1):

f₁ = 316.2 / (2 × 0.8) = 316.2/1.6 = 197.6 Hz

(c) 3rd Harmonic (n=3):

f₃ = 3f₁ = 3(197.6) = 592.8 Hz

Q10.4: Doppler Effect Calculation [5 marks]

A train blowing its whistle at frequency 400 Hz approaches a stationary observer on the platform at a speed of 36 km/h. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

(a) What is the apparent frequency heard by the observer as the train approaches?

(b) What is the apparent frequency heard after the train passes the observer?

(c) What is the change in frequency?

*

Concept:

We need to calculate two different frequencies:

1. Train Coming: Frequency shifts UP (High Pitch).

2. Train Going: Frequency shifts DOWN (Low Pitch).

The "change" is the difference between these two notes.

Formula:

  • Approach: f' = f [v / (v - vs)]
  • Recede: f'' = f [v / (v + vs)]

Calculation:

Input Data Table:

VariableSymbolValue
Source Velocityvs36 km/h (10 m/s)
Sound Speedv340 m/s
Source Frequencyfs400 Hz

(a) Approaching:

f' = 400 [340 / (340-10)] = 400(340/330)

f' = 412.1 Hz

(b) Receding:

f'' = 400 [340 / (340+10)] = 400(340/350)

f'' = 388.6 Hz

(c) Change:

Δf = 412.1 - 388.6 = 23.5 Hz

Q10.5: Speed of Sound & Laplace Correction [3 marks]

Write Newton's formula for the speed of sound in a gas and explain Laplace's correction.

*

Newton's Formula:

Assumed isothermal propagation:

v = √(P/ρ)

This gave a value (~280 m/s) much lower than experimental results for air (332 m/s).

Laplace's Correction:

Laplace suggested that sound propagation is an adiabatic process (rapid changes, no heat exchange).

Formula: v = √(γP/ρ)

Where γ = Cp/Cv (adiabatic index). For air, γ ≈ 1.4.

This corrected formula matches experimental values accurately.

Q10.6: Organ Pipes [5 marks]

Compare the fundamental frequencies of an open organ pipe and a closed organ pipe of the same length L.

*

1. Open Pipe (Open at both ends):

Wavelength λ = 2L

Frequency fopen = v / 2L

Harmonics: All harmonics present (f, 2f, 3f...)

2. Closed Pipe (Closed at one end):

Wavelength λ = 4L

Frequency fclosed = v / 4L

Harmonics: Only odd harmonics present (f, 3f, 5f...)

Comparison:

fopen / fclosed = (v/2L) / (v/4L) = 2

The fundamental frequency of an open pipe is twice that of a closed pipe of the same length.
