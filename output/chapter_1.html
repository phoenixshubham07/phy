A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q1.1: Scalars vs Vectors [3 marks]

Define scalar and vector quantities. Give three examples of each and explain why position is a vector but distance is a scalar.

* > Definition: > * Scalar: A quantity having only magnitude (size) and no direction. > * Vector: A quantity having both magnitude and direction, obeying vector laws.

Comparison Table:

| Feature | Scalar | Vector | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Magnitude | Yes | Yes | | Direction | No | Yes | | Addition | Simple Algebra (2+3=5) | Vector Algebra (Triangle Law) | | Example | Mass, Time, Speed | Force, Velocity, Position |

Relationship Explanation: * Distance vs. Position: * Distance (Scalar): "How much ground covered?" (e.g., 5 meters). Direction implies nothing. * Position (Vector): "Where are you relative to start?" (e.g., 5 meters *North*). Direction constitutes the definition. * *Analogy:* "Distance" is the odometer reading on your car. "Position" is the GPS pin on a map.

Q1.2: Vector Addition Methods [3 marks]

Explain the triangle law and parallelogram law of vector addition. When would you use each method?

* > Definition: > * Triangle Law: If two vectors are represented by two sides of a triangle in sequence, their sum is the third closing side. > * Parallelogram Law: If two vectors originate from a point, their sum is the diagonal of the parallelogram formed by them.

Usage Table:

| Method | Best Used For | Arrangement | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Triangle Law | Displacement, Sequential Motion | Head-to-Tail (Chain) | | Parallelogram Law | Forces, Simultaneous Pulls | Tail-to-Tail (Concurrent) |

Formula: $$ |R| = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + 2AB \cos \theta} $$

Q1.3: Dot Product Applications [3 marks]

What is the dot product of two vectors? Show how it's used to find the angle between them and explain why A·B = 0 means perpendicular vectors.

* > Definition: > The Dot Product (Scalar Product) is the product of the magnitudes of two vectors and the cosine of the angle between them. It measures "parallel projection".

Relationship Explanation: * Scalar Result: The output is a simple number, not an arrow. * Meaning: It tells you "How much of Vector A is pointing along Vector B?".

Formula: $$ \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = |\vec{A}| |\vec{B}| \cos \theta $$

Key Cases Table:

| Condition | Angle ($\theta$) | Dot Product | Interpretation | | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Parallel | $0^\circ$ | $+|A||B|$ | Maximum positive overlap | | Perpendicular | $90^\circ$ | $0$ | Orthogonal (No overlap) | | Anti-Parallel | $180^\circ$ | $-|A||B|$ | Maximum opposing overlap |

Finding Angle: $$ \theta = \cos^{-1} \left( \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{|\vec{A}| |\vec{B}|} \right) $$

Q1.4: Relative Velocity Concept [3 marks]

Define relative velocity. A boat moves at 10 m/s in still water. If the river current is 4 m/s, what does it mean when we say the boat's velocity relative to the ground? Draw a velocity diagram.

* > Definition: > Relative Velocity is the velocity of an object observed from a specific reference frame (which itself might be moving). It is the vector difference between velocities.

Formula: $$ \vec{v}_{rel} = \vec{v}_{object} - \vec{v}_{observer} $$

Boat Analysis: * Scenario: Boat tries to go North ($10\hat{j}$), River flows East ($4\hat{i}$). * Observer on Ground: Sees the boat moving diagonally because it participates in *both* motions. * Result Matrix:

| Component | Value | Source | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Vertical | 10 m/s | Engine | | Horizontal | 4 m/s | Current | | Resultant | $\sqrt{10^2+4^2}$ | Combined Motion |

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q1.5: Resultant of Two Vectors [5 marks]

Two forces of 12 N and 16 N act on a point at an angle of 60° to each other. Find (a) the magnitude of the resultant, (b) the angle it makes with the 12 N force, and (c) the angle it makes with the 16 N force.

* Concept: When two forces pull on an object at an angle, they don't just add up simply (12+16=28 is wrong!). We use a special formula (Law of Cosines) to find the "diagonal" or combined effect vector. To find the exact direction, we use the Sine Rule which relates angles to side lengths.

Formula: * R = √(F₁² + F₂² + 2F₁F₂ cosθ) * Direction (α): sin(α)/F₂ = sin(θ)/R

Calculation: Given: F₁ = 12 N, F₂ = 16 N, θ = 60°

(a) Magnitude: R = √(12² + 16² + 2(12)(16) cos60°) R = √(144 + 256 + 192) = √592 R = 24.33 N

(b) Angle with 12 N force (α): sin(α) = (16 × sin60°) / 24.33 = 13.856 / 24.33 = 0.569 α = 34.7°

(c) Angle with 16 N force (β): β = 60° - 34.7° β = 25.3°

Q1.6: River-Boat Problem [5 marks]

A boat capable of moving at 8 m/s in still water wants to cross a river of width 240 m. The current flows at 6 m/s. (a) In what direction should the boat head to cross perpendicular to the banks? (b) How long will it take to cross? (c) What is the resultant velocity of the boat?

* Concept: Think of crossing a moving conveyor belt. If you aim straight across, the belt drags you sideways. To go straight across (perpendicular), you must aim *upstream* (against the flow) so the current pushes you back onto the straight path. * Your engine pushes you diagonally. * The river pushes you downstream. * The result is you moving straight across.

Formula: * sinθ = vriver / vboat (Direction) * t = width / vperpendicular (Time)

Calculation: (a) Direction: sinθ = 6 / 8 = 0.75 θ = 48.6° upstream from vertical

(b) Time to Cross: v_⊥ = √(8² - 6²) = √28 = 5.29 m/s t = 240 / 5.29 t = 45.4 s

(c) Resultant Velocity: Since horizontal components cancel, vR = v_⊥ vR = 5.29 m/s

Q1.7: Relative Velocity - Two Objects [5 marks]

A car A travels due East at 60 km/h. Car B travels due North at 80 km/h. Both pass through the same point. Find the velocity of car B relative to car A and the angle it makes.

* Concept: Imagine you are sitting in Car A. You feel like you are still, and the whole world is moving past you. To calculate the velocity of Car B relative to you (vBA), you mathematically "stop" Car A by subtracting its velocity from Car B. * Rule: Relative Velocity = (Velocity of Object) - (Velocity of Observer).

Formula: vBA = vB - vA

Calculation: vA = 60i (East) vB = 80j (North) vBA = -60i + 80j

Magnitude: |vBA| = √((-60)² + 80²) = √10000 |vBA| = 100 km/h

Direction: tan φ = 80/60 = 4/3 φ = 53.1° North of West

Q1.8: Cross Product & Projection [5 marks]

Given vectors A = 3i + 4j and B = i + j. Find: (a) The cross product A × B. (b) The projection of A on B.

* Concept: * Cross Product: Creates a brand new vector that sticks out perpendicular to both original vectors (like the thumb in the Right-Hand Rule). * Projection: Think of it as a "shadow". It calculates the length of vector A's shadow falling onto line B.

Formula: * A × B = Determinant or Distributive Law * Projection = (A·B) / |B|

Calculation: (a) Cross Product: A × B = (3i + 4j) × (i + j) = 3(i×j) + 4(j×i) = 3k - 4k = -k

(b) Projection: A·B = 3(1) + 4(1) = 7 |B| = √(1²+1²) = √2 Projection = 7/√2 = 4.95
