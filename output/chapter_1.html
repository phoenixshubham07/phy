A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q1.1: Scalars vs Vectors [3 marks]

Define scalar and vector quantities. Give three examples of each and explain why position is a vector but distance is a scalar.

* Concept: Scalars imply magnitude only, while vectors possess both magnitude and direction. Position is a vector because it specifies location relative to an origin (direction matters), whereas distance is the total path length (direction irrelevant).

Formula: * Scalar: Magnitude * Vector: Magnitude + Direction

Key Examples: * Scalars: Mass, Speed, Energy. * Vectors: Velocity, Force, Displacement.

---

Q1.2: Vector Addition Methods [3 marks]

Explain the triangle law and parallelogram law of vector addition. When would you use each method?

* Concept: Vector addition rules depend on how vectors are arranged. Triangle law is for sequential vectors; Parallelogram law is for co-initial vectors.

Formula: * Triangle Law: R = A + B (Third side closing the triangle) * Resultant Magnitude: |R| = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ)

Application: 1. Triangle Law: Use for sequential vectors (head-to-tail). 2. Parallelogram Law: Use for simultaneous vectors from one point (tail-to-tail).

---

Q1.3: Dot Product Applications [3 marks]

What is the dot product of two vectors? Show how it's used to find the angle between them and explain why A·B = 0 means perpendicular vectors.

* Concept: The dot product is a scalar quantity obtained by multiplying magnitudes of two vectors with the cosine of the angle between them. It measures how much one vector extends in the direction of the other.

Formula: A · B = |A||B| cosθ

Key Points: * Finding Angle: cosθ = (A · B) / (|A| |B|) * Perpendicular Condition: If A · B = 0, then θ = 90° (Vectors are orthogonal).

---

Q1.4: Relative Velocity Concept [3 marks]

Define relative velocity. A boat moves at 10 m/s in still water. If the river current is 4 m/s, what does it mean when we say the boat's velocity relative to the ground? Draw a velocity diagram.

* Concept: Relative velocity is the velocity of an object as observed from a moving reference frame. It is the vector difference between individual velocities.

Formula: vrel = vobject - vobserver (Vector Subtraction)

Solution (Boat Example): * vboat = 10 m/s (in water) * vcurrent = 4 m/s (river) * Resultant Velocity: vR = vboat + vcurrent (since current carries boat)

Diagram: 10 m/s (Boat) ↑ | ________|____→ 4 m/s (Current) / / Resultant /

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q1.5: Resultant of Two Vectors [5 marks]

Two forces of 12 N and 16 N act on a point at an angle of 60° to each other. Find (a) the magnitude of the resultant, (b) the angle it makes with the 12 N force, and (c) the angle it makes with the 16 N force.

* Concept: The resultant of two vectors acting at an angle θ is found using the Law of Cosines. The direction is found using the Sine Rule.

Formula: * R = √(F₁² + F₂² + 2F₁F₂ cosθ) * Direction (α): sin(α)/F₂ = sin(θ)/R

Calculation: Given: F₁ = 12 N, F₂ = 16 N, θ = 60°

(a) Magnitude: R = √(12² + 16² + 2(12)(16) cos60°) R = √(144 + 256 + 192) = √592 R = 24.33 N

(b) Angle with 12 N force (α): sin(α) = (16 × sin60°) / 24.33 = 13.856 / 24.33 = 0.569 α = 34.7°

(c) Angle with 16 N force (β): β = 60° - 34.7° β = 25.3°

Q1.6: River-Boat Problem [5 marks]

A boat capable of moving at 8 m/s in still water wants to cross a river of width 240 m. The current flows at 6 m/s. (a) In what direction should the boat head to cross perpendicular to the banks? (b) How long will it take to cross? (c) What is the resultant velocity of the boat?

* Concept: To cross a river perpendicular to flow, the boat must head upstream such that the current cancels its horizontal component. Components of velocity are independent.

Formula: * sinθ = vriver / vboat (Direction) * t = width / vperpendicular (Time)

Calculation: (a) Direction: sinθ = 6 / 8 = 0.75 θ = 48.6° upstream from vertical

(b) Time to Cross: v_⊥ = √(8² - 6²) = √28 = 5.29 m/s t = 240 / 5.29 t = 45.4 s

(c) Resultant Velocity: Since horizontal components cancel, vR = v_⊥ vR = 5.29 m/s

Q1.7: Relative Velocity - Two Objects [5 marks]

A car A travels due East at 60 km/h. Car B travels due North at 80 km/h. Both pass through the same point. Find the velocity of car B relative to car A and the angle it makes.

* Concept: Relative velocity vBA is the velocity of B as seen by A. It is obtained by vector subtraction.

Formula: vBA = vB - vA

Calculation: vA = 60i (East) vB = 80j (North) vBA = -60i + 80j

Magnitude: |vBA| = √((-60)² + 80²) = √10000 |vBA| = 100 km/h

Direction: tan φ = 80/60 = 4/3 φ = 53.1° North of West

---

Q1.8: Cross Product & Projection [5 marks]

Given vectors A = 3i + 4j and B = i + j. Find: (a) The cross product A × B. (b) The projection of A on B.

* Concept: Cross product gives a perpendicular vector, Projection gives component length.

Formula: * A × B = Determinant or Distributive Law * Projection = (A·B) / |B|

Calculation: (a) Cross Product: A × B = (3i + 4j) × (i + j) = 3(i×j) + 4(j×i) = 3k - 4k = -k

(b) Projection: A·B = 3(1) + 4(1) = 7 |B| = √(1²+1²) = √2 Projection = 7/√2 = 4.95
