A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q4.1: Moment of Inertia [3 marks]

Define moment of inertia and explain how it's different from mass. Derive the moment of inertia of a uniform rod about one end.

* Concept: Momentum of Inertia (I) is just "Rotational Mass". * Heavy objects are hard to push (Mass). * Heavy spinning objects are hard to stop spinning (Momentum of Inertia). It depends not just on weight, but *where* the weight is. Weight far from the center = Harder to spin = Higher I.

Formula: * Torque: τ = Iα * Angular Momentum: L = Iω

Conservation: If τ_ext = 0, then Li = Lf. (I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂)

Q4.2: Rolling Motion on Incline [3 marks]

Explain rolling without slipping. Show that a rolling object down an incline has different velocity than a sliding object on a frictionless incline.

* Concept: Rolling is a special mix of two motions: Moving forward (Translation) + Spinning (Rotation). * "Without Slipping": Means the tire grips the road perfectly. The bottom point touching the ground is momentarily stuck (stationary), pushing the car forward. * Energy Split: When an object rolls down a hill, gravity's energy has to do two jobs: make it go fast AND make it spin. So it goes slower than a sliding block (which only needs to go fast).

Formula: * Condition: v = ωr * vrolling = √(2gh / (1+k)) where I=kmr²

Comparison: A rolling object stores energy in rotation, so less energy is available for speed compared to sliding. * vsliding = √(2gh) (Faster) * vrolling < vsliding (Slower)

Q4.3: Geostationary Satellites [3 marks]

What is a geostationary satellite? Explain the three conditions required for a satellite to be geostationary. Why is the orbital radius always 42,000 km?

* Concept: A Geostationary Satellite appears "frozen" in the sky to an observer on Earth. * How? It orbits at the *exact same speed* that Earth turns. Earth takes 24 hours to spin once; the satellite takes 24 hours to orbit once. * They move in sync, like two dancers holding hands.

Requirements: 1. T = 24 hours. 2. Equatorial Orbit. 3. Rotation: West to East.

Radius Formula: r = ∛[GMT²/(4π²)] ≈ 42,000 km. (Fixed value determined uniquely by T=24h).

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q4.4: Rolling Sphere on Incline [5 marks]

A uniform solid sphere of radius 0.2 m and mass 2 kg rolls without slipping down an incline of height 5 m. Find: (a) Velocity of center of mass at bottom (b) Angular velocity at bottom (c) Rotational kinetic energy at bottom (d) Translational kinetic energy at bottom

(For solid sphere, I = ⅖mr², g = 10 m/s²)

*

Given: r = 0.2 m, m = 2 kg, h = 5 m, I = ⅖mr² = ⅖(2)(0.2)² = 0.032 kg·m²

Solution: Concept: The ball starts at the top with Potential Energy (Height). As it rolls down, this energy turns into two things: 1. Speed (Translational KE): Moving forward. 2. Spin (Rotational KE): Spinning around. We use Conservation of Energy to find the final speed.

Formula: mgh = ½mv²(1 + k) * For sphere, k = 2/5 = 0.4.

Calculation: (a) Velocity: mgh = ½mv²(1.4) 10(5) = 0.7v² 50 = 0.7v² → v² = 71.4 v = 8.45 m/s

(b) Angular Velocity: ω = v/r = 8.45/0.2 = 42.25 rad/s

(c) Rotational KE: Krot = ½(⅖mr²)ω² = ⅕mv² = 0.2(2)(71.4) = 28.6 J

(d) Translational KE: Ktrans = ½mv² = 0.5(2)(71.4) = 71.4 J

Q4.5: Geostationary Satellite Orbital Velocity [5 marks]

Calculate the orbital velocity of a geostationary satellite. Use:

• Earth's mass: M = 6 × 10²⁴ kg
• G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
• Orbital radius: r = 4.2 × 10⁷ m

Also find the velocity change needed if the satellite moves to a polar orbit at radius 6.6 × 10⁶ m.

*

(a) Geostationary Orbital Velocity:

For circular orbit: v = √(GM/r)

vgeo = √(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6 × 10²⁴ / (4.2 × 10⁷))

vgeo = √(40.02 × 10¹³ / (4.2 × 10⁷))

vgeo = √(9.53 × 10⁶)

vgeo = 3,087 m/s = 3.09 km/s

Verification using T = 24 hours: v = 2πr/T = (2π × 4.2 × 10⁷) / 86,400 = 2.64 × 10⁸ / 86,400 = 3,056 m/s ✓ (Close match, slight difference due to rounding)

(b) Polar Orbit at Lower Altitude:

rpolar = 6.6 × 10⁶ m

vpolar = √(GM/rpolar)

vpolar = √(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6 × 10²⁴ / (6.6 × 10⁶))

vpolar = √(40.02 × 10¹³ / (6.6 × 10⁶))

vpolar = √(6.06 × 10⁶)

vpolar = 2,462 m/s = 2.46 km/s

(c) Change in Velocity: Δv = vgeo - vpolar = 3,087 - 2,462 = 625 m/s

This is the reduction in orbital velocity when moving to lower orbit (higher speed needed to be closer to Earth for same period).

Note: Actually, for a polar orbit to be stable, we need different equations, but this shows the basic orbital mechanics principle: closer orbits require lower velocities.

Q4.6: Moment of Inertia Calculation [5 marks]

A uniform disk of radius R = 0.3 m and mass M = 5 kg is rotated about its center. A point mass m = 1 kg is placed at distance r = 0.2 m from the axis. Find: (a) Moment of inertia of disk alone (b) Moment of inertia of point mass (c) Total moment of inertia (d) Angular acceleration if a torque of 2 N·m is applied

*

(a) Moment of Inertia of Uniform Disk: For disk about center: Idisk = ½MR² = ½(5)(0.3)² = ½(5)(0.09) = 0.225 kg·m²

(b) Moment of Inertia of Point Mass: Imass = mr² = 1 × (0.2)² = 0.04 kg·m²

(c) Total Moment of Inertia: Itotal = Idisk + Imass = 0.225 + 0.04 = 0.265 kg·m²

(d) Angular Acceleration: Using τ = Iα: α = τ / Itotal = 2 / 0.265 = 7.55 rad/s²

Physical Interpretation:

• The disk contributes 85% of total rotational inertia (0.225/0.265)
• The point mass contributes only 15%
• Even though the point mass is small, its effect on angular acceleration is significant
• The system resists angular acceleration with I = 0.265 kg·m²

Q4.8: Parallel and Perpendicular Axis Theorems [5 marks]

State the Theorem of Parallel Axes and Theorem of Perpendicular Axes for Moment of Inertia.

* Concept: Calculating Moment of Inertia (I) for weird axes can be hard. These two theorems are "Shortcuts": * Parallel Axis Theorem: If you know 'I' through the center, you can easily find 'I' for any other parallel axis (like swinging a bat from the handle vs center). * Perpendicular Axis Theorem: For flat objects (like a pizza), the spin inertia around the vertical axis is just the sum of the other two axes.

Formula: * Parallel Axis: I = Icm + Md² * Perpendicular Axis: Iz = Ix + Iy (Planar objects only)

Definitions: * Icm: MOI through Center of Mass. * d: perpendicular distance between axes.

Q4.7: Center of Mass [3 marks]

Define Centre of Mass (CM). Give the formula for the position of the centre of mass of a two-body system.

* Definition: The Center of Mass is the "Balance Point" of an object. * If you balance a pen on your finger, your finger is at the CM. * In physics, we pretend the *entire gravity* pulls on just this one single point to simplify math.

Formula for Two-Body System: For two masses m₁ and m₂ at positions r₁ and r₂: Rcm = (m₁r₁ + m₂r₂) / (m₁ + m₂)

Significance:

• If m₁ >> m₂, the CM is closer to m₁.
• External forces act as if applied at the CM.
