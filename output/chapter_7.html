A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q7.1: Lens Formula Derivation [3 marks]

Derive the lens formula. Explain the sign conventions used.

* Answer: Concept: The Lens Formula is the rulebook for how lenses bend light. It connects three distances: 1. Where the object is (`u`). 2. Where the image appears (`v`). 3. How strong the lens is (`f`). Sign Convention: Think of the lens as the origin `(0,0)` of a graph. Light moves Left → Right. Anything "against" the light (left) is negative; anything "with" the light (right) is positive.

Formula: * Lens Formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u * Magnification: m = v/u = h'/h

Key Conventions: 1. Light travels Left to Right. 2. Origin at Optical Center. 3. Real Object: u is negative. 4. Convex Focal Length: Positive. 5. Concave Focal Length: Negative.

---

Q7.2: Mirror Formula and Types of Images [3 marks]

Derive the mirror formula and compare the images formed by concave and convex mirrors.

* Answer: Concept: * Concave Mirror: Like the inside of a spoon. It collects light to a point. It can make images big, small, upside down, or right side up, depending on where you stand. * Convex Mirror: Like the back of a spoon. It spreads light out. It *always* makes you look smaller and shorter, but it lets you see a huge area behind you (perfect for cars).

Formula: * Mirror Formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u * Magnification: m = -v/u

Comparison: * Concave: Can form Real, Inverted, Enlarged images. Used in torches, shaving mirrors. * Convex: Always Virtual, Erect, Diminished. Used as Rear-view mirror (Wide field of view).

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q7.3: Convex Lens - Image Formation [5 marks]

A convex lens of focal length 15 cm forms an image of an object placed at: (a) 45 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 10 cm

For each case, find image distance, magnification, and nature of image.

*

Solution: Concept: Convex lenses are "Converging"—they focus light. * The Rule: The closer the object gets to the focus (f), the farther and bigger the image gets. * Virtual Image: If the object gets *too* close (closer than f), the lens acts like a magnifying glass.

Formula: * 1/v - 1/u = 1/f → 1/v = 1/f + 1/u * m = v/u

Calculation: f = +15 cm.

(a) u = -45 cm: 1/v = 1/15 - 1/45 = 2/45 v = +22.5 cm (Real, Inverted) m = 22.5/-45 = -0.5 (Diminished)

(b) u = -20 cm: 1/v = 1/15 - 1/20 = 1/60 v = +60 cm (Real, Inverted) m = 60/-20 = -3 (Enlarged)

(c) u = -10 cm: 1/v = 1/15 - 1/10 = -1/30 v = -30 cm (Virtual, Erect) m = -30/-10 = +3 (Magnified)

---

Q7.4: Concave Mirror - Real and Virtual Images [5 marks]

A concave mirror of focal length 20 cm is used to form images. Find image position and magnification when object is at: (a) 60 cm (b) 30 cm (c) 10 cm

*

Solution: Concept: Concave mirrors are tricky! * Far away object: Image is real, inverted (upside down), and in front of the mirror. * Very close object: Image becomes virtual, erect (right side up), and magnified (like a makeup mirror). * Key Math: Always set object distance `u` as negative.

Formula: * 1/v + 1/u = 1/f * m = -v/u

Calculation: f = -20 cm (Concave).

(a) u = -60 cm: 1/v = -1/20 + 1/60 = -2/60 v = -30 cm (Real, Inverted) [In front] m = -(-30/-60) = -0.5

(b) u = -30 cm: 1/v = -1/20 + 1/30 = -1/60 v = -60 cm (Real, Inverted) m = -(-60/-30) = -2

(c) u = -10 cm: 1/v = -1/20 + 1/10 = 1/20 v = +20 cm (Virtual, Erect) [Behind] m = -(20/-10) = +2

Q7.5: Combination of Lenses [3 marks]

What happens to the focal length and power when two thin lenses are placed in contact? Give the formula.

* Answer: Concept: When you stack two thin lenses together, they act like one super-lens. * Power Addition: You simply add their "powers" together. Two weak reading glasses stacked = One strong reading glass. * Formula: `Ptotal = P1 + P2`. Since Power is inverse of focal length (P=1/f), the formula is `1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2`.

Formula: * Focal Length: 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂ * Power: P = P₁ + P₂

Significance: Allows creation of compound lenses to correct aberrations (like achromatic doublets).

Q7.6: Poiseuille's Law - Flow Rate [3 marks]

How does the flow rate of a viscous fluid through a cylindrical pipe change if the radius is doubled? What if the length is doubled?

* Concept: Flow rate is defined by Poiseuille's Law. It depends on r⁴ and inversely on length/viscosity.

Formula: Q = (π P r⁴) / (8 η L)

Analysis: * Double r (r→2r): Flow increases 2⁴ = 16 times. * Double L (L→2L): Flow decreases by half (0.5 times).

Conclusion: Small radius changes drastically affect flow rate (physiologically important in blood vessels).
