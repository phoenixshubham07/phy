A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q7.1: Lens Formula Derivation [3 marks]

Derive the lens formula. Explain the sign conventions used.

*

> Lens Formula:

> A mathematical relationship relating the distance of object (\(u\)), image (\(v\)), and focal length (\(f\)).

>

\[ \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \]

Sign Convention (Cartesian):

DirectionSignLogic
Incident Light\(\rightarrow\)Direction of light is Positive X
Object Distance\(-\)Measured against light (Left)
Real Image\(+\)Measured with light (Right)
Convex Focus\(+\)Converging
Concave Focus\(-\)Diverging

Derivation Geometry:

  • Triangles formed by Object-Center and Image-Center are Similar.
  • Ratio of Heights = Ratio of Distances (\(h'/h = v/u\)).
  • Combining with Focal point triangles yields the formula.

Q7.2: Mirror Formula and Types of Images [3 marks]

Derive the mirror formula and compare the images formed by concave and convex mirrors.

*

> Mirror Formula:

>

\[ \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \]

Image Comparison Table:

Mirror TypeReal Image?Virtual Image?SizeApplication
ConcaveYes (if far)Yes (if very close)Enlarged/DiminishedShaving Mirror, Telescope
ConvexNoYes (Always)Always DiminishedCar Rear-View Mirror

Visual Logic:

  • Concave: "Cave in". Traps light, focuses it. Can flip images.
  • Convex: "Flex out". Scatters light. Captures a wide field of view.

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q7.3: Convex Lens - Image Formation [5 marks]

A convex lens of focal length 15 cm forms an image of an object placed at:

(a) 45 cm

(b) 20 cm

(c) 10 cm

For each case, find image distance, magnification, and nature of image.

*

Solution:

Concept:

Convex lenses are "Converging"—they focus light.

  • The Rule: The closer the object gets to the focus (f), the farther and bigger the image gets.
  • Virtual Image: If the object gets too close (closer than f), the lens acts like a magnifying glass.

Formula:

  • 1/v - 1/u = 1/f → 1/v = 1/f + 1/u
  • m = v/u

Calculation:

f = +15 cm.

(a) u = -45 cm:

1/v = 1/15 - 1/45 = 2/45

v = +22.5 cm (Real, Inverted)

m = 22.5/-45 = -0.5 (Diminished)

(b) u = -20 cm:

1/v = 1/15 - 1/20 = 1/60

v = +60 cm (Real, Inverted)

m = 60/-20 = -3 (Enlarged)

(c) u = -10 cm:

1/v = 1/15 - 1/10 = -1/30

v = -30 cm (Virtual, Erect)

m = -30/-10 = +3 (Magnified)

Q7.4: Concave Mirror - Real and Virtual Images [5 marks]

A concave mirror of focal length 20 cm is used to form images. Find image position and magnification when object is at:

(a) 60 cm

(b) 30 cm

(c) 10 cm

*

Solution:

Concept:

Concave mirrors are tricky!

  • Far away object: Image is real, inverted (upside down), and in front of the mirror.
  • Very close object: Image becomes virtual, erect (right side up), and magnified (like a makeup mirror).
  • Key Math: Always set object distance `u` as negative.

Formula:

  • 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
  • m = -v/u

Calculation:

f = -20 cm (Concave).

(a) u = -60 cm:

1/v = -1/20 + 1/60 = -2/60

v = -30 cm (Real, Inverted) [In front]

m = -(-30/-60) = -0.5

(b) u = -30 cm:

1/v = -1/20 + 1/30 = -1/60

v = -60 cm (Real, Inverted)

m = -(-60/-30) = -2

(c) u = -10 cm:

1/v = -1/20 + 1/10 = 1/20

v = +20 cm (Virtual, Erect) [Behind]

m = -(20/-10) = +2

Q7.5: Combination of Lenses [3 marks]

What happens to the focal length and power when two thin lenses are placed in contact? Give the formula.

*

Answer:

Concept:

When you stack two thin lenses together, they act like one super-lens.

  • Power Addition: You simply add their "powers" together. Two weak reading glasses stacked = One strong reading glass.
  • Formula: `Ptotal = P1 + P2`. Since Power is inverse of focal length (P=1/f), the formula is `1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2`.

Formula:

  • Focal Length: 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂
  • Power: P = P₁ + P₂

Significance:

Allows creation of compound lenses to correct aberrations (like achromatic doublets).

Q7.6: Poiseuille's Law - Flow Rate [3 marks]

How does the flow rate of a viscous fluid through a cylindrical pipe change if the radius is doubled? What if the length is doubled?

*

Concept:

Flow rate is defined by Poiseuille's Law. It depends on r⁴ and inversely on length/viscosity.

Formula:

Q = (π P r⁴) / (8 η L)

Analysis:

  • Double r (r→2r): Flow increases 2⁴ = 16 times.
  • Double L (L→2L): Flow decreases by half (0.5 times).

Conclusion:

Small radius changes drastically affect flow rate (physiologically important in blood vessels).
