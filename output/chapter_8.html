A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q8.1: Bernoulli's Principle [3 marks]

State Bernoulli's principle and derive Bernoulli's equation. Explain its assumptions.

* Bernoulli's Principle: For an ideal fluid flowing along a streamline, the sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy per unit volume remains constant.

Bernoulli's Equation: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant (along a streamline)

Or, between two points: P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂

With head terms: (P/ρg) + (v²/2g) + h = constant

Where:

• P/ρg = pressure head
• v²/2g = velocity head
• h = gravitational head

Derivation:

Consider fluid element moving along streamline. Work-energy theorem: Wtotal = ΔKE + ΔPE

Work by pressure forces: Wpressure = P₁A₁L₁ - P₂A₂L₂

Using continuity: A₁L₁ = A₂L₂ = ΔV (volume) Wpressure = (P₁ - P₂)ΔV

Change in KE: ΔKE = ½m(v₂² - v₁²) = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²)

Change in PE: ΔPE = mgΔh = ρgΔVΔh

Applying work-energy theorem: (P₁ - P₂)ΔV = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔVΔh

Dividing by ΔV: P₁ - P₂ = ½ρ(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔh

Rearranging: P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂ ✓

Assumptions:

1. Incompressible fluid: Density ρ is constant 2. Non-viscous: No friction/viscous forces 3. Steady flow: Velocity at any point doesn't change with time 4. Along a streamline: Valid only for points on same streamline 5. No external work: No pumps or turbines

Physical Meaning:

Where velocity is high → pressure is low (and vice versa)

This explains:

• Airplane wing lift (fast air over top = lower pressure)
• Water jet from pipe narrows (higher velocity in narrower region)
• Venturi effect (pressure decreases in narrow section)

Q8.2: Continuity Equation [3 marks]

Derive the continuity equation. Show that A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ for incompressible fluid flow.

* Continuity Equation (Conservation of Mass):

For incompressible, steady flow through a pipe: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ (mass flow rate is constant)

Or: A·v = constant (volumetric flow rate is constant)

Derivation:

Consider a pipe with varying cross-section. In time dt, fluid travels distances:

• At section 1: L₁ = v₁ dt
• At section 2: L₂ = v₂ dt

Volume entering at section 1: V₁ = A₁ × L₁ = A₁ × v₁ × dt

Volume leaving at section 2: V₂ = A₂ × L₂ = A₂ × v₂ × dt

For incompressible fluid (ρ = constant): Mass entering = Mass leaving

ρA₁v₁dt = ρA₂v₂dt

Canceling ρdt: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ ✓

Volumetric Flow Rate: Q = A × v = constant

Units: m³/s

Physical Interpretation:

When pipe narrows:

• A decreases → v must increase
• Faster flow in narrow sections
• Example: Water spraying far from a nozzle (narrower opening → faster water)

When pipe widens:

• A increases → v must decrease
• Slower flow in wide sections

Combined with Bernoulli's Equation:

P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

In narrow section:

• v is large (from continuity)
• P must be small (from Bernoulli)
• This is why pressure drops in narrow sections

Practical Applications: 1. Carburetors: Narrow passages create low pressure to draw fuel 2. Airplane wings: Curved top surface accelerates air (low pressure = lift) 3. Water meters: Constriction increases velocity, pressure drop indicates flow

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q8.3: Bernoulli's Equation Application [5 marks]

Water flows through a horizontal pipe that narrows from diameter 10 cm to 5 cm. The pressure at the wide section is 50 kPa and velocity is 1 m/s. Find: (a) Velocity in narrow section (b) Pressure in narrow section (c) Volumetric flow rate (d) Why does pressure decrease in the narrow section?

*

Given:

• Wide section: D₁ = 10 cm, r₁ = 5 cm, P₁ = 50 kPa = 50,000 Pa, v₁ = 1 m/s
• Narrow section: D₂ = 5 cm, r₂ = 2.5 cm
• ρ = 1000 kg/m³ (water)

(a) Velocity in Narrow Section:

Using continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

A₁ = πr₁² = π(0.05)² = 0.00785 m² A₂ = πr₂² = π(0.025)² = 0.00196 m²

v₂ = A₁v₁/A₂ = (0.00785 × 1)/0.00196 v₂ = 0.00785/0.00196 = 4 m/s

Check: (0.05/0.025)² = 2² = 4 (area ratio inverse = velocity ratio) ✓

(b) Pressure in Narrow Section:

Using Bernoulli's equation (horizontal pipe, h₁ = h₂): P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²

P₂ = P₁ + ½ρ(v₁² - v₂²) P₂ = 50,000 + ½(1000)(1² - 4²) P₂ = 50,000 + 500(1 - 16) P₂ = 50,000 + 500(-15) P₂ = 50,000 - 7,500 P₂ = 42,500 Pa = 42.5 kPa

Pressure drops by 7.5 kPa (15% drop)

(c) Volumetric Flow Rate:

Q = A₁v₁ = 0.00785 × 1 = 0.00785 m³/s = 7.85 L/s

Or: Q = A₂v₂ = 0.00196 × 4 = 0.00784 ≈ 0.00785 m³/s ✓

(d) Why Pressure Decreases:

From Bernoulli's principle:

• As water enters narrow section, it must speed up (continuity)
• Higher speed → higher kinetic energy (½ρv²)
• Total energy must be constant
• Therefore, pressure energy (P) must decrease
• Pressure converts to kinetic energy

This is characteristic of Bernoulli's principle:

• Pressure head + Velocity head + Elevation head = constant
• High velocity region has low pressure
• Low velocity region has high pressure

Q8.4: Buoyancy & Archimedes' Principle [3 marks]

State Archimedes' Principle. What is the formula for the buoyant force?

* Archimedes' Principle: A body wholly or partially submerged in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces.

Formula: Fb = ρ g V

Where:

• Fb = Buoyant force
• ρ = Density of the fluid
• g = Acceleration due to gravity
• V = Volume of fluid displaced (volume of submerged part of body)

---

Q8.5: Pascal's Law [3 marks]

State Pascal's Law. Give one application.

* Statement: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and the walls of the containing vessel.

Formula: P = F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂

Application: Hydraulic Lift: A small force applied on a small piston creates a large pressure, which translates to a large force on a larger piston, effectively multiplying the force. Used in car brakes and heavy lifting.

---

Q8.6: Pressure at Depth [3 marks]

How does pressure vary with depth in a static fluid? Give the formula.

* Variation with Depth: Pressure increases with depth due to the weight of the liquid column above.

Formula: P = P₀ + ρgh

Where:

• P = Pressure at depth h
• P₀ = Atmospheric pressure (at surface)
• ρ = Density of fluid
• g = Acceleration due to gravity
• h = Depth below surface

Q8.7: Hydraulic Lift Problem [5 marks]

In a hydraulic lift, the input piston has a radius of 1 cm and the output piston has a radius of 10 cm. A force of 50 N is applied to the input piston. (a) What force is exerted by the output piston? (b) If the input piston moves down 10 cm, how much does the output piston move up?

* (a) Force Output: According to Pascal's Law: P₁ = P₂ => F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂ Areas: A₁ = π(1)², A₂ = π(10)² = 100π F₂ = F₁ × (A₂/A₁) = 50 × (100π / π) = 50 × 100 F₂ = 5000 N (Force multiplication!)

(b) Distance Moved: Volume displaced must be equal (Conservation of Loop): A₁d₁ = A₂d₂ d₂ = d₁ × (A₁/A₂) = 10 × (1/100) d₂ = 0.1 cm
