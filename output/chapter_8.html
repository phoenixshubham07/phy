A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

Q8.1: Bernoulli's Principle [3 marks]

State Bernoulli's principle and derive Bernoulli's equation. Explain its assumptions.

* > Principle: > For an ideal fluid streamline, the sum of Pressure Energy, Kinetic Energy, and Potential Energy per unit volume is constant. > "Where speed is high, pressure is low."

Bernoulli Equation:

![Bernoulli Equation](/Users/phoenixechoes/.gemini/antigravity/brain/4c8b2949-d571-4420-ba84-c87141bf969f/uploadedimage_1765030547511.png)

Assumptions Table:

| Assumption | Meaning | Why needed? | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Incompressible | Density $\rho$ is constant | No squeezing allowed | | Non-Viscous | No friction | No energy lost to heat | | Streamline Flow | Smooth path | No turbulence | | Steady Flow | Velocity constant at a point | Time independent |

Energy Trade-off Explanation: * Fluid has a fixed "Energy Budget". * If it spends budget on Speed ($v \uparrow$), it must reduce spending on Pressure ($P \downarrow$). * *Application:* Airplane wings (Fast air on top = Low Pressure = Lift).

Derivation: Consider fluid element moving along streamline. Work-energy theorem: Wtotal = ΔKE + ΔPE

Work by pressure forces: Wpressure = P₁A₁L₁ - P₂A₂L₂

Using continuity: A₁L₁ = A₂L₂ = ΔV (volume) Wpressure = (P₁ - P₂)ΔV

Change in KE: ΔKE = ½m(v₂² - v₁²) = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²)

Change in PE: ΔPE = mgΔh = ρgΔVΔh

Applying work-energy theorem: (P₁ - P₂)ΔV = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔVΔh

Dividing by ΔV: P₁ - P₂ = ½ρ(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔh

Rearranging: P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂ ✓

Assumptions:

1. Incompressible fluid: Density ρ is constant 2. Non-viscous: No friction/viscous forces 3. Steady flow: Velocity at any point doesn't change with time 4. Along a streamline: Valid only for points on same streamline 5. No external work: No pumps or turbines

Physical Meaning:

Where velocity is high → pressure is low (and vice versa)

This explains:

• Airplane wing lift (fast air over top = lower pressure)
• Water jet from pipe narrows (higher velocity in narrower region)
• Venturi effect (pressure decreases in narrow section)

Q8.2: Continuity Equation [3 marks]

Derive the continuity equation. Show that A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ for incompressible fluid flow.

* > Concept: > Conservation of Mass. The amount of water entering a pipe must equal the amount leaving it (if no leaks).

Flow Relationship:

| Pipe Section | Area ($A$) | Speed ($v$) | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Wide | Large | Slow | | Narrow | Small | Fast |

Formula: $$ A1 v1 = A2 v2 $$

Derivation: 1. Mass In = Mass Out 2. $\rho A1 (v1 \Delta t) = \rho A2 (v2 \Delta t)$ 3. Cancel $\rho$ and $\Delta t$. 4. Result: $A1 v1 = A2 v2 = Q$ (Flow Rate).

B. NUMERICAL QUESTIONS (5 marks each)

Q8.3: Bernoulli's Equation Application [5 marks]

Water flows through a horizontal pipe that narrows from diameter 10 cm to 5 cm. The pressure at the wide section is 50 kPa and velocity is 1 m/s. Find: (a) Velocity in narrow section (b) Pressure in narrow section (c) Volumetric flow rate (d) Why does pressure decrease in the narrow section?

* Concept: We use two main tools: 1. Continuity: "Pipe gets narrower → Water travels faster." 2. Bernoulli: "Water travels faster → Pressure drops." So, in the narrow part of the pipe, we expect high speed and low pressure.

Formula: * Continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ * Bernoulli (horizontal pipe): P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂² * Volumetric Flow Rate: Q = Av

Calculation: Input Data Table:

| Parameter | Wide Section (1) | Narrow Section (2) | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Diameter ($D$) | 10 cm (0.1 m) | 5 cm (0.05 m) | | Radius ($r$) | 5 cm (0.05 m) | 2.5 cm (0.025 m) | | Pressure ($P$) | 50 kPa | ? | | Velocity ($v$) | 1 m/s | ? | *Density of water $\rho = 1000$ kg/m³*

(a) Velocity in Narrow Section (v₂): Using continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ Since A = πr², then πr₁²v₁ = πr₂²v₂ r₁²v₁ = r₂²v₂ v₂ = v₁ × (r₁/r₂)² v₂ = 1 m/s × (0.05 m / 0.025 m)² v₂ = 1 m/s × (2)² v₂ = 4 m/s

(b) Pressure in Narrow Section (P₂): Using Bernoulli's equation (horizontal pipe, h₁ = h₂): P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂² P₂ = P₁ + ½ρ(v₁² - v₂²) P₂ = 50,000 Pa + ½(1000 kg/m³)((1 m/s)² - (4 m/s)²) P₂ = 50,000 + 500(1 - 16) P₂ = 50,000 + 500(-15) P₂ = 50,000 - 7,500 P₂ = 42,500 Pa = 42.5 kPa

(c) Volumetric Flow Rate (Q): Q = A₁v₁ = πr₁²v₁ Q = π(0.05 m)² × 1 m/s Q = π(0.0025) m³/s Q ≈ 0.00785 m³/s (or 7.85 L/s)

(d) Why does pressure decrease in the narrow section? Explanation: From Bernoulli's principle, the total energy (pressure energy + kinetic energy + potential energy) along a streamline remains constant.

• As the water flows into the narrower section, its velocity must increase due to the continuity equation (conservation of mass).
• An increase in velocity means an increase in the kinetic energy (½ρv²) of the fluid.
• Since the pipe is horizontal, the potential energy (ρgh) remains constant.
• To keep the total energy constant, the pressure energy (P) must decrease to compensate for the increase in kinetic energy.
• Therefore, pressure converts to kinetic energy in the narrow section.

Q8.4: Buoyancy & Archimedes' Principle [3 marks]

State Archimedes' Principle. What is the formula for the buoyant force?

* Archimedes' Principle: A body wholly or partially submerged in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces.

Formula: Fb = ρ g V

Where:

• Fb = Buoyant force
• ρ = Density of the fluid
• g = Acceleration due to gravity
• V = Volume of fluid displaced (volume of submerged part of body)

Q8.5: Pascal's Law [3 marks]

State Pascal's Law. Give one application.

* Statement: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and the walls of the containing vessel.

Formula: P = F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂

Application: Hydraulic Lift: A small force applied on a small piston creates a large pressure, which translates to a large force on a larger piston, effectively multiplying the force. Used in car brakes and heavy lifting.

Q8.6: Pressure at Depth [3 marks]

How does pressure vary with depth in a static fluid? Give the formula.

* Variation with Depth: Pressure increases with depth due to the weight of the liquid column above.

Formula: P = P₀ + ρgh

Where:

• P = Pressure at depth h
• P₀ = Atmospheric pressure (at surface)
• ρ = Density of fluid
• g = Acceleration due to gravity
• h = Depth below surface

Q8.7: Hydraulic Lift Problem [5 marks]

In a hydraulic lift, the input piston has a radius of 1 cm and the output piston has a radius of 10 cm. A force of 50 N is applied to the input piston. (a) What force is exerted by the output piston? (b) If the input piston moves down 10 cm, how much does the output piston move up?

* (a) Force Output: Pascal's Law: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂ F₂ = F₁ × (A₂/A₁) Ratio of Areas = (r₂/r₁)² = (10/1)² = 100 F₂ = 50 × 100 = 5000 N

(b) Distance Moved: Volume Conserved: A₁d₁ = A₂d₂ d₂ = d₁ × (A₁/A₂) = 10 × (1/100) d₂ = 0.1 cm
