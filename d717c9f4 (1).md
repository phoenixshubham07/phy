# 1. VECTOR ALGEBRA & RELATIVE VELOCITY

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)



### Q1.1: Scalars vs Vectors [3 marks]
**Question:** Define scalar and vector quantities. Give three examples of each and explain why position is a vector but distance is a scalar.



**Answer:**

> **Definition:**
> *   **Scalar:** A quantity having only magnitude (size) and no direction.
> *   **Vector:** A quantity having both magnitude and direction, obeying vector laws.

**Comparison Table:**

| Feature | Scalar | Vector |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Magnitude** | Yes | Yes |
| **Direction** | No | Yes |
| **Addition** | Simple Algebra (2+3=5) | Vector Algebra (Triangle Law) |
| **Example** | Mass, Time, Speed | Force, Velocity, Position |

**Relationship Explanation:**
*   **Distance vs. Position:**
    *   **Distance (Scalar):** "How much ground covered?" (e.g., 5 meters). Direction implies nothing.
    *   **Position (Vector):** "Where are you relative to start?" (e.g., 5 meters *North*). Direction constitutes the definition.
    *   *Analogy:* "Distance" is the odometer reading on your car. "Position" is the GPS pin on a map.

### Q1.2: Vector Addition Methods [3 marks]
**Question:** Explain the triangle law and parallelogram law of vector addition. When would you use each method?



**Answer:**

> **Definition:**
> *   **Triangle Law:** If two vectors are represented by two sides of a triangle in sequence, their sum is the third closing side.
> *   **Parallelogram Law:** If two vectors originate from a point, their sum is the diagonal of the parallelogram formed by them.



**Usage Table:**

| Method | Best Used For | Arrangement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Triangle Law** | Displacement, Sequential Motion | Head-to-Tail (Chain) |
| **Parallelogram Law** | Forces, Simultaneous Pulls | Tail-to-Tail (Concurrent) |


**Formula:**

$$ |R| = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + 2AB \cos \theta} $$

### Q1.3: Dot Product Applications [3 marks]
**Question:** What is the dot product of two vectors? Show how it's used to find the angle between them and explain why A·B = 0 means perpendicular vectors.



**Answer:**

> **Definition:**
> The **Dot Product** (Scalar Product) is the product of the magnitudes of two vectors and the cosine of the angle between them. It measures "parallel projection".

**Relationship Explanation:**
*   **Scalar Result:** The output is a simple number, not an arrow.
*   **Meaning:** It tells you "How much of Vector A is pointing along Vector B?".


**Formula:**

$$ \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = |\vec{A}| |\vec{B}| \cos \theta $$

**Key Cases Table:**

| Condition | Angle ($\theta$) | Dot Product | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Parallel** | $0^\circ$ | $+|A||B|$ | Maximum positive overlap |
| **Perpendicular** | $90^\circ$ | $0$ | **Orthogonal (No overlap)** |
| **Anti-Parallel** | $180^\circ$ | $-|A||B|$ | Maximum opposing overlap |

**Finding Angle:**
$$ \theta = \cos^{-1} \left( \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{|\vec{A}| |\vec{B}|} \right) $$

### Q1.4: Relative Velocity Concept [3 marks]
**Question:** Define relative velocity. A boat moves at 10 m/s in still water. If the river current is 4 m/s, what does it mean when we say the boat's velocity relative to the ground? Draw a velocity diagram.



**Answer:**

> **Definition:**
> **Relative Velocity** is the velocity of an object observed from a specific reference frame (which itself might be moving). It is the vector difference between velocities.




**Formula:**

$$ \vec{v}_{rel} = \vec{v}_{object} - \vec{v}_{observer} $$

**Boat Analysis:**
*   **Scenario:** Boat tries to go North ($10\hat{j}$), River flows East ($4\hat{i}$).
*   **Observer on Ground:** Sees the boat moving diagonally because it participates in *both* motions.
*   **Result Matrix:**

| Component | Value | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vertical** | 10 m/s | Engine |
| **Horizontal** | 4 m/s | Current |
| **Resultant** | $\sqrt{10^2+4^2}$ | Combined Motion |

### Q1.5: Resultant of Two Vectors [5 marks]
**Question:** Two forces of 12 N and 16 N act on a point at an angle of 60° to each other. Find (a) the magnitude of the resultant, (b) the angle it makes with the 12 N force, and (c) the angle it makes with the 16 N force.



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

When two forces pull on an object at an angle, they don't just add up simply (12+16=28 is wrong!). We use a special formula (Law of Cosines) to find the "diagonal" or combined effect vector. To find the exact direction, we use the Sine Rule which relates angles to side lengths.


**Formula:**

*   R = √(F₁² + F₂² + 2F₁F₂ cosθ)
*   Direction (α): sin(α)/F₂ = sin(θ)/R


**Calculation:**

Given: F₁ = 12 N, F₂ = 16 N, θ = 60°

**(a) Magnitude:**
R = √(12² + 16² + 2(12)(16) cos60°)
R = √(144 + 256 + 192) = √592
**R = 24.33 N**

**(b) Angle with 12 N force (α):**
sin(α) = (16 × sin60°) / 24.33 = 13.856 / 24.33 = 0.569
**α = 34.7°**

**(c) Angle with 16 N force (β):**
β = 60° - 34.7°
**β = 25.3°**

### Q1.6: River-Boat Problem [5 marks]
**Question:** A boat capable of moving at 8 m/s in still water wants to cross a river of width 240 m. The current flows at 6 m/s. 
(a) In what direction should the boat head to cross perpendicular to the banks?
(b) How long will it take to cross?
(c) What is the resultant velocity of the boat?



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

Think of crossing a moving conveyor belt. If you aim straight across, the belt drags you sideways. To go straight across (perpendicular), you must aim *upstream* (against the flow) so the current pushes you back onto the straight path.
*   Your engine pushes you diagonally.
*   The river pushes you downstream.
*   The result is you moving straight across.


**Formula:**

*   sinθ = v_river / v_boat (Direction)
*   t = width / v_perpendicular (Time)


**Calculation:**

**(a) Direction:**
sinθ = 6 / 8 = 0.75
**θ = 48.6° upstream from vertical**

**(b) Time to Cross:**
v_⊥ = √(8² - 6²) = √28 = 5.29 m/s
t = 240 / 5.29
**t = 45.4 s**

**(c) Resultant Velocity:**
Since horizontal components cancel, v_R = v_⊥
**v_R = 5.29 m/s**

### Q1.7: Relative Velocity - Two Objects [5 marks]
**Question:** A car A travels due East at 60 km/h. Car B travels due North at 80 km/h. Both pass through the same point. Find the velocity of car B relative to car A and the angle it makes.



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

Imagine you are sitting in Car A. You feel like you are still, and the whole world is moving past you. To calculate the velocity of Car B relative to you (**v_BA**), you mathematically "stop" Car A by subtracting its velocity from Car B.
*   **Rule:** Relative Velocity = (Velocity of Object) - (Velocity of Observer).


**Formula:**

**v_BA** = **v_B** - **v_A**


**Calculation:**

**v_A** = 60**i** (East)
**v_B** = 80**j** (North)
**v_BA** = -60**i** + 80**j**

**Magnitude:**
|**v_BA**| = √((-60)² + 80²) = √10000
**|v_BA| = 100 km/h**

**Direction:**
tan φ = 80/60 = 4/3
**φ = 53.1° North of West**

### Q1.8: Cross Product & Projection [5 marks]
**Question:** Given vectors **A** = 3**i** + 4**j** and **B** = **i** + **j**. Find:
(a) The cross product **A** × **B**.
(b) The projection of **A** on **B**.



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

*   **Cross Product:** Creates a brand new vector that sticks out **perpendicular** to both original vectors (like the thumb in the Right-Hand Rule).
*   **Projection:** Think of it as a "shadow". It calculates the length of vector A's shadow falling onto line B.


**Formula:**

*   **A** × **B** = Determinant or Distributive Law
*   Projection = (**A**·**B**) / |**B**|


**Calculation:**

**(a) Cross Product:**
**A** × **B** = (3**i** + 4**j**) × (**i** + **j**)
= 3(**i**×**j**) + 4(**j**×**i**) = 3**k** - 4**k**
**= -k**

**(b) Projection:**
**A**·**B** = 3(1) + 4(1) = 7
|**B**| = √(1²+1²) = √2
**Projection = 7/√2 = 4.95**

# 2. PROJECTILE MOTION

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)



### Q2.1: Principle of Independence of Motion [3 marks]
**Question:** Explain the principle of independence of motion in projectile motion. Why can horizontal and vertical motions be analyzed separately?



**Answer:**

> **Principle:**
> The horizontal and vertical components of projectile motion are **completely independent** of each other. Motion along one axis has zero effect on motion along the perpendicular axis.

**Axis Analysis Table:**

| Axis | Force | Acceleration | Motion Type | Equation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Horizontal (X)** | None (0) | $a_x = 0$ | **Uniform Velocity** | $x = u_x t$ |
| **Vertical (Y)** | Gravity ($mg$) | $a_y = -g$ | **Uniform Acceleration** | $y = u_y t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$ |

**Reasoning:**
*   Forces are vectors.
*   Gravity acts purely vertically (down). It has **no component** horizontally ($\cos 90^\circ = 0$).
*   Therefore, the horizontal speed never changes (ignoring air), while the vertical speed changes constantly.

---



### Q2.2: Maximum Height and Time of Flight [3 marks]
**Question:** Derive the expressions for maximum height and time of flight of a projectile launched at angle θ with initial velocity u.



**Answer:**



> **Definitions:**
> *   **Max Height ($H$):** Vertical distance where $v_y = 0$.
> *   **Time of Flight ($T$):** Total duration the projectile remains in air ($y_{final} = 0$).

**Derivation Steps:**

| Parameter | Condition Used | Equation Derived |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Max Height ($H$)** | At peak, vertical velocity $v_y = 0$ | $$ H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g} $$ |
| **Time of Flight ($T$)** | Total vertical displacement $y = 0$ | $$ T = \frac{2u \sin \theta}{g} $$ |

**Detailed Proof:**
1.  **For H:** Use $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$.
    *   $0^2 - (u \sin \theta)^2 = 2(-g)H \implies H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}$
2.  **For T:** Use $y = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.
    *   $0 = (u \sin \theta)T - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 \implies T = \frac{2u \sin \theta}{g}$

---



### Q2.3: Range of Projectile [3 marks]
**Question:** Show that the range of a projectile is maximum at 45° and why complementary angles (θ and 90°-θ) give the same range.



**Answer:**

> **Definition (Range):**
> Horizontal distance covered during the time of flight. Determined by the product of horizontal velocity and total time.


**Formula:**

$$ R = \frac{u^2 \sin 2\theta}{g} $$

**Logic Table:**

| Case | Angle | Mathematical Reason | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Max Range** | $\theta = 45^\circ$ | $\sin 2\theta$ reaches max value (1) at $90^\circ$ ($2\times45$). | $R_{max} = u^2/g$ |
| **Complementary** | $90^\circ - \theta$ | $\sin(2(90-\theta)) = \sin(180-2\theta) = \sin 2\theta$ | Ranges are **Equal** |

**Interpretation:**
*   **45°:** The perfect compromise between "Going High" (Time) and "Going Fast Forward" (Speed).
*   **Complementary:** A high lob ($60^\circ$) and a low drive ($30^\circ$) land at the same spot, though their flight times differ.

### Q2.4: Basic Projectile Calculation [5 marks]
**Question:** A projectile is fired horizontally with initial velocity 40 m/s from the top of a cliff 80 m high. Find:
(a) Time of flight
(b) Horizontal range
(c) Velocity when it hits the ground
(d) Angle of velocity at impact



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

**Horizontal Projectile Motion:** Launch angle is 0°.
*   Initial Velocity: $u_x = 40, u_y = 0$.
*   Motion acts like "Free Fall" + "Constant Drift".

**Input Data Table:**

| Variable | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Initial Velocity ($u_x$) | 40 m/s |
| Height ($h$) | 80 m |
| Gravity ($g$) | 10 m/s² |
| Initial Vertical ($u_y$) | 0 m/s |

**Calculations:**

**(a) Time of Flight ($t$):**
Using $h = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$:
$$ 80 = \frac{1}{2}(10)t^2 \implies 16 = t^2 \implies \mathbf{t = 4 \text{ s}} $$

**(b) Horizontal Range ($R$):**
$$ R = u_x \times t = 40 \times 4 = \mathbf{160 \text{ m}} $$

**(c) Final Velocity ($v$):**
*   $v_x = 40$ m/s (Constant)
*   $v_y = gt = 10(4) = 40$ m/s
*   Resultant: $v = \sqrt{40^2 + 40^2} = \mathbf{56.57 \text{ m/s}} $

**(d) Angle ($\alpha$):**
$$ \tan \alpha = \frac{v_y}{v_x} = \frac{40}{40} = 1 \implies \mathbf{\alpha = 45^\circ} $$

### Q2.5: Projectile at an Angle [5 marks]
**Question:** A projectile is launched at 30° to the horizontal with initial speed 50 m/s. Find:
(a) Maximum height reached
(b) Time to reach maximum height
(c) Horizontal range
(d) Time of flight
(Use g = 10 m/s²)



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

This is a standard "cannonball" problem. We take the launch velocity (50 m/s) and split it into two parts:
*   **Upward part (u sinθ):** Fights gravity, decides height and time.
*   **Forward part (u cosθ):** Drives the distance (range).


**Formula:**

*   H = (u² sin²θ) / 2g
*   R = (u² sin2θ) / g
*   T = (2u sinθ) / g


**Calculation:**

Given: u=50, θ=30°, g=10.

**(a) Max Height:**
H = (50² × 0.5²) / 20 = 625/20 = **31.25 m**

**(b) Time to Max Height:**
t = (u sinθ)/g = (50×0.5)/10 = **2.5 s**

**(c) Range:**
R = (2500 × sin60°)/10 = 250(0.866) = **216.5 m**

**(d) Total Time:**
T = 2 × 2.5 = **5 s**

### Q2.6: Projectile - Height and Range Problem [5 marks]
**Question:** A ball is projected at angle 37° to the horizontal with speed 20 m/s. A wall of height 5 m stands at horizontal distance 30 m. Will the ball clear the wall? If yes, by how much? (sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8, g = 10 m/s²)



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

We need to find the height (y) of the ball exactly when it is 30m away (x=30).
Think of the path as a curve (parabola). We use the **Equation of Trajectory** which gives us the height 'y' for any horizontal distance 'x'. If at x=30, the ball's height y is more than 5m, it clears the wall!


**Formula:**

Trajectory Equation: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)


**Calculation:**

x = 30, θ = 37°, u = 20.

y = 30(0.75) - [10(900)] / [2(400)(0.64)]
y = 22.5 - 9000/512
y = 22.5 - 17.58
**y = 4.92 m**

**Conclusion:**
Wall height = 5 m. Projectile height = 4.92 m.
**Ball falls short by 0.08 m (Hits the wall).**

### Q2.7: Two-Stage Projectile Motion [5 marks]
**Question:** A cricketer throws a ball at 35° to the horizontal with initial speed 25 m/s. Another cricketer standing at horizontal distance 60 m wants to catch it at the same height from which it was thrown. At what height should he position his hands if he can catch a ball at heights from ground level up to 2.2 m?



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

The catch is that the second cricketer is standing **60m away**, but we calculate the ball's natural range first. If the ball naturally lands at 58m, it will never reach the guy at 60m in the air! It will bounce on the ground first. So the "catch height" is effectively ground level.


**Formula:**

y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)


**Calculation:**

x = 60 m, u = 25 m/s, θ = 35°.
Actual Range R = (625 × 0.94)/10 = 58.75 m.

Since target distance 60 m > Range 58.75 m, the ball hits the ground before reaching the fielder.
**Height at x=60 m is effectively 0 (Ground level).**

**Conclusion:**
Fielder can catch it at ground level (0 m) if he moves slightly forward to 58.75 m.

### Q2.8: Trajectory Equation Derivation [5 marks]
**Question:** Derive the equation of the trajectory of a projectile: y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ). Prove that the path is parabolic.



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

We usually have two equations: one for x (distance) involving time 't', and one for y (height) involving time 't'.
To see the *shape* of the path, we need to remove 't' from the picture. By plugging the time from x-equation into the y-equation, we get a single formula linking Height (y) directly to Distance (x). This formula looks like `y = ax - bx²`, which is the mathematical shape of a Parabola.


**Formula:**

*   x = (u cosθ)t
*   y = (u sinθ)t - ½gt²

**Derivation:**
1.  From x-eqn: t = x / (u cosθ)
2.  Substitute into y-eqn:
    y = (u sinθ)[x / (u cosθ)] - ½g[x / (u cosθ)]²
    **y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u²cos²θ)**

**Conclusion:**
This calculates a parabola ($y = ax - bx^2$).

# 3. WORK, ENERGY & MOMENTUM

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)



### Q3.1: Work by Variable Force [3 marks]
**Question:** Define work done by a variable force. How is it different from constant force? Explain using a spring as an example.



**Answer:**

> **Definition:**
> Work is the energy transferred to or from an object via the application of force along a displacement. For a variable force, it is the integration of force over position.

**Comparison Table:**

| Feature | Constant Force | Variable Force |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Force Nature** | Uniform magnitude | Changes with position ($x$) |
| **Calculation** | Multiplication ($W = F \cdot s$) | Calculus ($W = \int F dx$) |
| **Example** | Pushing a box on smooth floor | Stretching a spring ($F = -kx$) |

**Spring Example:**
*   As you stretch a spring, it fights back harder (Force increases).
*   We sum up the work done for every tiny millimeter of stretch.
*   Result: $W = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$ (Area under F-x graph).

---



### Q3.2: Work-Energy Theorem [3 marks]
**Question:** State and derive the work-energy theorem. Explain what it means physically.



**Answer:**

> **Theorem:**
> The **Net Work** done on an object by all forces acts to change its **Kinetic Energy**.
> $$ W_{net} = \Delta K = K_f - K_i $$

**Derivation Flow:**

1.  **Newton's Second Law:** $F = ma$
2.  **Definition of Work:** $W = \int F dx = \int m a dx$
3.  **Chain Rule Trick:** $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dx} \frac{dx}{dt} = v \frac{dv}{dx}$
4.  **Substitution:**
    $$ W = \int m \left( v \frac{dv}{dx} \right) dx = \int m v dv $$
5.  **Integration:**
    $$ W = m \left[ \frac{v^2}{2} \right]_{u}^{v} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2 $$

**Physical Meaning:**
It connects **Force** (Cause) acting over distance to **Speed** (Effect). If you do positive work, you speed it up. Negative work slows it down.

---



### Q3.3: Impulse and Momentum [3 marks]
**Question:** Define impulse and explain how it relates to momentum change. Why do airbags in cars extend the time of impact?



**Answer:**

> **Definition:**
> **Impulse ($J$)** is the product of average force and the time interval during which it acts. It equals the total Change in Momentum.


**Formula:**

$$ J = \int F dt = \mathbf{\Delta p} $$

**Real World Application: Airbags**
*   **Goal:** Survivor must come to rest ($\Delta p$ is fixed).
*   **Relation:** $F_{avg} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$.
*   **Mechanism:** Airbags are soft; they increase the collision time ($\Delta t$) significantly.
*   **Result:** Since $\Delta t$ goes UP, Impact Force ($F_{avg}$) comes DOWN.

**Analogy:**
Jumping onto concrete (instant stop, high pain) vs. Jumping onto a mattress (slow stop, no pain).

---



### Q3.4: Momentum Conservation in Collisions [3 marks]
**Question:** State the law of conservation of momentum. Explain why momentum is conserved in collisions but kinetic energy is not conserved in inelastic collisions.



**Answer:**

> **Law of Conservation of Momentum:**
> In an isolated system (no external forces), the total momentum remains constant before and after a collision.
> $$ p_{initial} = p_{final} $$

**Collision Types Table:**

| Collision Type | Momentum | Kinetic Energy | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Elastic** | Conserved | Conserved | No deformation or heat loss. |
| **Inelastic** | Conserved | **Not Conserved** | Energy lost to **Deformation, Heat, Sound**. |

**Reasoning:**
Momentum is conserved because internal forces (Newton's 3rd Law) cancel out. Energy is not conserved in inelastic collisions because "crumpling a clear" or "making a loud bang" costs energy!

### Q3.5: Work by Variable Force [5 marks]
**Question:** A force F = 3x + 2 (in Newtons, where x is in meters) acts on a 2 kg object. Find the work done when the object moves from x = 0 to x = 4 m. Also find the final velocity if it starts from rest.



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

We are given a force `F = 3x + 2` that changes as the object moves (depends on x).
1.  **Find Work:** Since force changes, we integrate (sum up) the force over the distance.
2.  **Find Speed:** Use the Work-Energy Theorem: "Total Work Done = Kinetic Energy Gained".


**Formula:**

*   W = ∫ F dx
*   W = ½mv²


**Calculation:**

**(a) Work Done:**
W = ∫₀⁴ (3x + 2) dx = [1.5x² + 2x]₀⁴
W = 1.5(16) + 2(4) = 24 + 8 = **32 J**

**(b) Final Velocity:**
32 = ½(2)v²
v² = 32
**v = 5.66 m/s**

### Q3.6: Momentum Conservation - Perfectly Inelastic Collision [5 marks]
**Question:** A 3 kg object moving at 8 m/s collides head-on with a 2 kg object moving at 5 m/s in the opposite direction. They stick together after collision. Find:
(a) Velocity and direction after collision
(b) Loss in kinetic energy
(c) Energy converted to other forms



**Solution:**


**Given:**
- Object 1: m₁ = 3 kg, u₁ = 8 m/s (let's say rightward, positive)
- Object 2: m₂ = 2 kg, u₂ = -5 m/s (leftward, opposite, negative)



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

"Perfectly Inelastic" implies the objects crash and **stick together** like wet clay. They move as one single blob afterwards.
*   **Conservation:** Momentum is still conserved! Total momentum before = Total momentum of the "blob" after.


**Formula:**

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v


**Calculation:**

**(a) Final Velocity:**
3(8) + 2(-5) = (3+2)v
24 - 10 = 5v
14 = 5v → **v = 2.8 m/s**

**(b) Energy Loss:**
K_i = ½(3)(64) + ½(2)(25) = 96 + 25 = 121 J
K_f = ½(5)(2.8)² = 19.6 J
Loss = 121 - 19.6 = **101.4 J**

### Q3.7: Elastic Collision [5 marks]
**Question:** A 4 kg ball moving at 6 m/s elastically collides with a 2 kg ball initially at rest. Find the velocities of both balls after collision.



**Solution:**


**Given:**
- m₁ = 4 kg, u₁ = 6 m/s
- m₂ = 2 kg, u₂ = 0



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

"Elastic" implies a bouncy collision (like superballs).
*   No energy is lost to heat or deformation.
*   We use **two** rules: (1) Conservation of Momentum AND (2) Conservation of Kinetic Energy. This lets us solve for the two unknown final speeds.


**Formula:**

*   m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
*   v₁ = [(m₁-m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂] / (m₁+m₂)


**Calculation:**

m₁=4, u₁=6; m₂=2, u₂=0.

v₁ = [(2)(6)] / 6 = **2 m/s**
v₂ = [2(4)(6)] / 6 = **8 m/s**

### Q3.8: Impulse-Momentum Problem [5 marks]
**Question:** A car of mass 1500 kg moving at 20 m/s hits a wall. The collision lasts 0.1 seconds. The car comes to rest. Find:
(a) Change in momentum
(b) Average force during collision
(c) If with an airbag the collision time extends to 0.5 seconds, what is the new average force?
(d) How many times is the force reduced?



**Solution:**

### Q6.3: Young's Double Slit - Fringe Width [5 marks]
**Question:** In Young's double-slit experiment, light of wavelength 600 nm is used. Two slits are separated by 0.2 mm. The screen is 1 m away. Find:
(a) Fringe width
(b) Distance of 3rd bright fringe from center
(c) Distance of 4th dark fringe from center
(d) Intensity at a point where path difference is λ/4



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

In Young's Experiment, light shines through two tiny slits. The waves fan out and overlap on a screen.
*   Where they match up, you see stripes of Light (**Fringes**).
*   Where they cancel, you see Darkness.
*   **Fringe Width:** The distance between two bright stripes. It tells you how "zoomed in" the pattern is.


**Formula:**

*   Fringe Width: β = λD/d
*   Position of n-th Bright Fringe: y_n_bright = nβ
*   Position of n-th Dark Fringe: y_n_dark = (n - 0.5)β
*   Intensity at a point: I = I_max cos²(δ/2) where δ = (2π/λ)Δx (Δx is path difference)


**Calculation:**

**Input Data Table:**

| Variable | Symbol | Value | Converted SI |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wavelength** | $\lambda$ | 600 nm | $6 \times 10^{-7}$ m |
| **Slit Separation** | $d$ | 0.2 mm | $2 \times 10^{-4}$ m |
| **Screen Distance** | $D$ | 1 m | 1 m |

**(a) Fringe Width (β):**
β = (λD) / d = (6 × 10⁻⁷ m × 1 m) / (2 × 10⁻⁴ m)
β = 3 × 10⁻³ m = **3 mm**

**(b) Distance of 3rd Bright Fringe from center (y₃_bright):**
For bright fringes, n = 3.
y₃_bright = 3β = 3 × (3 mm) = **9 mm**

**(c) Distance of 4th Dark Fringe from center (y₄_dark):**
For dark fringes, n = 4.
y₄_dark = (4 - 0.5)β = 3.5β = 3.5 × (3 mm) = **10.5 mm**

**(d) Intensity at a point where path difference Δx = λ/4:**
First, calculate the phase difference (δ):
δ = (2π/λ)Δx = (2π/λ)(λ/4) = π/2 radians

Assuming I_max = 4I₀ (where I₀ is intensity from a single slit):
I = I_max cos²(δ/2) = 4I₀ cos²((π/2)/2) = 4I₀ cos²(π/4)
I = 4I₀ (1/√2)² = 4I₀ (1/2) = **2I₀**

### Q8.1: Bernoulli's Principle [3 marks]
**Question:** State Bernoulli's principle and derive Bernoulli's equation. Explain its assumptions.



**Answer:**

> **Principle:**
> For an ideal fluid streamline, the sum of Pressure Energy, Kinetic Energy, and Potential Energy per unit volume is constant.
> "Where speed is high, pressure is low."

**Bernoulli Equation:**



**Assumptions Table:**

| Assumption | Meaning | Why needed? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Incompressible** | Density $\rho$ is constant | No squeezing allowed |
| **Non-Viscous** | No friction | No energy lost to heat |
| **Streamline Flow** | Smooth path | No turbulence |
| **Steady Flow** | Velocity constant at a point | Time independent |

**Energy Trade-off Explanation:**
*   Fluid has a fixed "Energy Budget".
*   If it spends budget on **Speed** ($v \uparrow$), it must reduce spending on **Pressure** ($P \downarrow$).
*   *Application:* Airplane wings (Fast air on top = Low Pressure = Lift).

**Derivation:**
Consider fluid element moving along streamline.
Work-energy theorem:
W_total = ΔKE + ΔPE

Work by pressure forces:
W_pressure = P₁A₁L₁ - P₂A₂L₂

Using continuity: A₁L₁ = A₂L₂ = ΔV (volume)
W_pressure = (P₁ - P₂)ΔV

Change in KE:
ΔKE = ½m(v₂² - v₁²) = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²)

Change in PE:
ΔPE = mgΔh = ρgΔVΔh

Applying work-energy theorem:
(P₁ - P₂)ΔV = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔVΔh

Dividing by ΔV:
P₁ - P₂ = ½ρ(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔh

Rearranging:
**P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂** ✓

**Assumptions:**

1. **Incompressible fluid:** Density ρ is constant
2. **Non-viscous:** No friction/viscous forces
3. **Steady flow:** Velocity at any point doesn't change with time
4. **Along a streamline:** Valid only for points on same streamline
5. **No external work:** No pumps or turbines

**Physical Meaning:**

Where velocity is high → pressure is low (and vice versa)

This explains:
- Airplane wing lift (fast air over top = lower pressure)
- Water jet from pipe narrows (higher velocity in narrower region)
- Venturi effect (pressure decreases in narrow section)

### Q8.2: Continuity Equation [3 marks]
**Question:** Derive the continuity equation. Show that A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ for incompressible fluid flow.



**Answer:**

> **Concept:**
> **Conservation of Mass.** The amount of water entering a pipe must equal the amount leaving it (if no leaks).

**Flow Relationship:**

| Pipe Section | Area ($A$) | Speed ($v$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wide** | Large | Slow |
| **Narrow** | Small | Fast |


**Formula:**

$$ A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 $$

**Derivation:**
1.  Mass In = Mass Out
2.  $\rho A_1 (v_1 \Delta t) = \rho A_2 (v_2 \Delta t)$
3.  Cancel $\rho$ and $\Delta t$.
4.  **Result:** $A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 = Q$ (Flow Rate).

### Q8.3: Bernoulli's Equation Application [5 marks]
**Question:** Water flows through a horizontal pipe that narrows from diameter 10 cm to 5 cm. The pressure at the wide section is 50 kPa and velocity is 1 m/s. Find:
(a) Velocity in narrow section
(b) Pressure in narrow section
(c) Volumetric flow rate
(d) Why does pressure decrease in the narrow section?



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

We use two main tools:
1.  **Continuity:** "Pipe gets narrower → Water travels faster."
2.  **Bernoulli:** "Water travels faster → Pressure drops."
So, in the narrow part of the pipe, we expect high speed and low pressure.


**Formula:**

*   Continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
*   Bernoulli (horizontal pipe): P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²
*   Volumetric Flow Rate: Q = Av


**Calculation:**

**Input Data Table:**

| Parameter | Wide Section (1) | Narrow Section (2) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diameter ($D$)** | 10 cm (0.1 m) | 5 cm (0.05 m) |
| **Radius ($r$)** | 5 cm (0.05 m) | 2.5 cm (0.025 m) |
| **Pressure ($P$)** | 50 kPa | ? |
| **Velocity ($v$)** | 1 m/s | ? |
*Density of water $\rho = 1000$ kg/m³*

**(a) Velocity in Narrow Section (v₂):**
Using continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
Since A = πr², then πr₁²v₁ = πr₂²v₂
r₁²v₁ = r₂²v₂
v₂ = v₁ × (r₁/r₂)²
v₂ = 1 m/s × (0.05 m / 0.025 m)²
v₂ = 1 m/s × (2)²
v₂ = **4 m/s**

**(b) Pressure in Narrow Section (P₂):**
Using Bernoulli's equation (horizontal pipe, h₁ = h₂):
P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²
P₂ = P₁ + ½ρ(v₁² - v₂²)
P₂ = 50,000 Pa + ½(1000 kg/m³)((1 m/s)² - (4 m/s)²)
P₂ = 50,000 + 500(1 - 16)
P₂ = 50,000 + 500(-15)
P₂ = 50,000 - 7,500
P₂ = **42,500 Pa = 42.5 kPa**

**(c) Volumetric Flow Rate (Q):**
Q = A₁v₁ = πr₁²v₁
Q = π(0.05 m)² × 1 m/s
Q = π(0.0025) m³/s
Q ≈ **0.00785 m³/s** (or 7.85 L/s)

**(d) Why does pressure decrease in the narrow section?**
**Explanation:**
From Bernoulli's principle, the total energy (pressure energy + kinetic energy + potential energy) along a streamline remains constant.
- As the water flows into the narrower section, its velocity must increase due to the continuity equation (conservation of mass).
- An increase in velocity means an increase in the kinetic energy (½ρv²) of the fluid.
- Since the pipe is horizontal, the potential energy (ρgh) remains constant.
- To keep the total energy constant, the pressure energy (P) must decrease to compensate for the increase in kinetic energy.
- Therefore, **pressure converts to kinetic energy** in the narrow section.

### Q8.5: Pascal's Law [3 marks]
**Question:** State Pascal's Law. Give one application.



**Answer:**

**Statement:**
Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and the walls of the containing vessel.


**Formula:**

**P = F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂**

**Application:**
**Hydraulic Lift:** A small force applied on a small piston creates a large pressure, which translates to a large force on a larger piston, effectively multiplying the force. Used in car brakes and heavy lifting.

### Q8.7: Hydraulic Lift Problem [5 marks]
**Question:** In a hydraulic lift, the input piston has a radius of 1 cm and the output piston has a radius of 10 cm. A force of 50 N is applied to the input piston.

# 9. THERMODYNAMICS

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)



### Q9.1: First Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]
**Question:** State and explain the first law of thermodynamics. Define internal energy, heat, and work.



**Answer:**

> **First Law:**
> The Law of Conservation of Energy applied to thermodynamic systems.
> $$ \Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W $$

**Term Definitions:**

| Symbol | Term | Positive Means... | Negative Means... |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\Delta Q$ | **Heat** | Heat **Added** to system | Heat **Removed** |
| $\Delta W$ | **Work** | Work done **BY** system (Expansion) | Work done **ON** system (Compression) |
| $\Delta U$ | **Internal Energy** | Temperature **Increases** | Temperature **Decreases** |


**Concept:**

"Energy In ($Q$) = Energy Stored ($U$) + Energy Used ($W$)"
This can also be written as: **ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV** (for constant pressure)

**Definitions:**

   - **Negative (Q < 0):** Heat released by system
   - Path function (depends on how energy is transferred)

3. **Work (W):**
   - Energy transferred by mechanical means (expansion/compression)
   - **Positive (W > 0):** Work done BY system (expansion)
   - **Negative (W < 0):** Work done ON system (compression)
   - W = PΔV for constant pressure

**First Law Interpretation:**

ΔQ = ΔU + W
- Heat input = Increase in internal energy + Work done by system
- **Energy conservation:** Total energy input must equal total output

**Example Processes:**

1. **Isothermal (T = constant):**
   - ΔU = 0 (temperature constant)
   - ΔQ = W (all heat converts to work)

2. **Adiabatic (ΔQ = 0):**
   - 0 = ΔU + W
   - ΔU = -W (work done reduces internal energy, temperature drops)

3. **Isochoric (V = constant):**
   - W = 0 (no expansion)
   - ΔQ = ΔU (all heat increases internal energy)

4. **Isobaric (P = constant):**
   - W = PΔV
   - ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV

### Q9.2: Second Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]
**Question:** State the second law of thermodynamics in two forms. Explain why perfect heat engines are impossible.



**Answer:**


**Concept:**

**The Second Law** is about "Real Life vs. Theory".
*   **Heat Flow:** Heat naturally flows Hot → Cold. You need a machine (fridge) to force it the other way.
*   **Efficiency:** You can't turn 100% of heat into work. Some heat is *always* wasted (dumped into the cold sink). That's why perpetual motion machines are impossible.

**Statement:**
*   **Kelvin-Planck:** No cyclic engine can convert heat completely into work. (Efficiency < 100%).
*   **Clausius:** Heat cannot flow spontaneously from cold to hot.

**Why Impossible:**
A perfect engine implies ΔS_total < 0 for the universe, which violates the Entropy principle (ΔS ≥ 0).

**Question:** One mole of an ideal gas at pressure 10 atm and volume 2 L undergoes isothermal expansion to 10 L at temperature 300 K. Find:

(b) Heat absorbed

W = (1)(8.314)(300) ln(10/2)
W = 2494.2 × 1.609 = **4013 J**

**(b) Heat Absorbed:**
Since ΔU = 0, ΔQ = W = **4013 J**

**(c) Internal Energy:**
**ΔU = 0** (Isothermal)

**(d) Verification:**
4013 = 0 + 4013 (First Law Holds).

(c) Find work output
(d) Find heat rejected to cold reservoir

### Q10.5: Speed of Sound & Laplace Correction [3 marks]
**Question:** Write Newton's formula for the speed of sound in a gas and explain Laplace's correction.



**Answer:**

**Newton's Formula:**
Assumed isothermal propagation:
v = √(P/ρ)
This gave a value (~280 m/s) much lower than experimental results for air (332 m/s).

**Laplace's Correction:**

This corrected formula matches experimental values accurately.

### Q9.6: Mayer's Relation (Cp - Cv) [5 marks]
**Question:** Derive the relation Cp - Cv = R for an ideal gas.



**Answer:**


**Concept:**

Why are there two specific heats (Cp and Cv)?
*   **Cv (Constant Volume):** You heat a box that *can't expand*. All heat goes into raising temperature. (Cheap).
*   **Cp (Constant Pressure):** You heat a box that *expands*. The heat has to do TWO jobs: raise temperature AND do work to push the walls out. (Expensive).
*   So **Cp > Cv** (specifically, Cp - Cv = R).

**Derivation:**
1.  **First Law:** ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV
2.  At Constant V: ΔW=0 → ΔQ_v = ΔU = nCvΔT
3.  At Constant P: ΔQ_p = ΔU + PΔV = nCpΔT
4.  Since PΔV = nRΔT (Ideal Gas Law):
    nCpΔT = nCvΔT + nRΔT
    **Cp - Cv = R**

> **Definition:**
> The apparent change in frequency of a wave due to relative motion between the source and the observer.

| Action | Frequency Change | Pitch Perception |

### Q10.4: Doppler Effect Calculation [5 marks]
**Question:** A train blowing its whistle at frequency 400 Hz approaches a stationary observer on the platform at a speed of 36 km/h. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.
(a) What is the apparent frequency heard by the observer as the train approaches?
(b) What is the apparent frequency heard after the train passes the observer?
(c) What is the change in frequency?



**Solution:**


**Concept:**

We need to calculate two different frequencies:
1.  **Train Coming:** Frequency shifts UP (High Pitch).
2.  **Train Going:** Frequency shifts DOWN (Low Pitch).
The "change" is the difference between these two notes.


**Formula:**

*   Approach: f' = f [v / (v - v_s)]
*   Recede: f'' = f [v / (v + v_s)]


**Calculation:**

**Input Data Table:**

| Variable | Symbol | Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source Velocity** | $v_s$ | 36 km/h (10 m/s) |
| **Sound Speed** | $v$ | 340 m/s |
| **Source Frequency** | $f_s$ | 400 Hz |

**(a) Approaching:**
f' = 400 [340 / (340-10)] = 400(340/330)
**f' = 412.1 Hz**

**(b) Receding:**
f'' = 400 [340 / (340+10)] = 400(340/350)
**f'' = 388.6 Hz**

**(c) Change:**
Δf = 412.1 - 388.6 = **23.5 Hz**
