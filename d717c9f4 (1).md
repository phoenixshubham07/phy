# 1. VECTOR ALGEBRA & RELATIVE VELOCITY

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

### Q1.1: Scalars vs Vectors [3 marks]
**Question:** Define scalar and vector quantities. Give three examples of each and explain why position is a vector but distance is a scalar.

**Answer:**
**Scalars:** Physical quantities that have only magnitude, no direction.
- Examples: Mass (5 kg), Temperature (30°C), Speed (25 m/s), Energy (100 J), Density (800 kg/m³)

**Vectors:** Physical quantities that have both magnitude and direction.
- Examples: Displacement (50 m, North), Velocity (25 m/s, East), Force (100 N, downward), Acceleration (9.8 m/s², downward), Momentum (p = mv, in direction of motion)

**Why position is a vector but distance is a scalar:**
- **Distance:** The total length of path traveled—only tells "how much" without direction. It's always positive and path-dependent.
- **Position:** Describes "where" an object is relative to a reference point with both magnitude AND direction. A person at 50 m North vs 50 m South is at different positions despite same distance from origin.

### Q1.2: Vector Addition Methods [3 marks]
**Question:** Explain the triangle law and parallelogram law of vector addition. When would you use each method?

**Answer:**
**Triangle Law of Vector Addition:**
- When two vectors **A** and **B** are represented as two sides of a triangle, their resultant **R** = **A** + **B** is represented by the third side of the triangle.
- The resultant vector is drawn from the starting point of the first vector to the endpoint of the second vector.
- **Formula:** |**R**| = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ)
- **Use when:** Adding vectors in sequence (e.g., displacement in two stages)

**Parallelogram Law of Vector Addition:**
- When two vectors **A** and **B** are represented as adjacent sides of a parallelogram, their resultant **R** is represented by the diagonal of the parallelogram.
- Both vectors start from the same point.
- **Use when:** Two forces/velocities/displacements act simultaneously from the same point

**Key Difference:** Triangle law is sequential (one after another), parallelogram law is simultaneous (from same point).

### Q1.3: Dot Product Applications [3 marks]
**Question:** What is the dot product of two vectors? Show how it's used to find the angle between them and explain why A·B = 0 means perpendicular vectors.

**Answer:**
**Dot Product Definition:**
The dot product of vectors **A** and **B** is: **A** · **B** = AB cosθ

Where A and B are magnitudes, θ is the angle between them.

**Algebraic Form:** **A** · **B** = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z

**Finding Angle Between Vectors:**
cosθ = (**A** · **B**) / (|**A**| |**B**|)
θ = cos⁻¹[(**A** · **B**) / (|**A**| |**B**|)]

**Why A·B = 0 means perpendicular:**
If **A** · **B** = AB cosθ = 0, and A ≠ 0, B ≠ 0, then cosθ = 0
This means θ = 90°, indicating perpendicular vectors.

**Geometric Interpretation:** Dot product measures how much one vector "points in the direction" of another. If they point at right angles, there's zero projection, hence zero dot product.

### Q1.4: Relative Velocity Concept [3 marks]
**Question:** Define relative velocity. A boat moves at 10 m/s in still water. If the river current is 4 m/s, what does it mean when we say the boat's velocity relative to the ground? Draw a velocity diagram.

**Answer:**
**Relative Velocity Definition:** The velocity of an object as observed from another moving reference frame.

**Boat Problem Setup:**
- **v_boat (in water)** = 10 m/s (boat's speed relative to water)
- **v_river (current)** = 4 m/s (water's speed relative to ground)
- **v_boat relative to ground** = **v_boat** + **v_river** (vector addition)

**Velocity Diagram:**
```
        v_boat = 10 m/s (upriver)
             ↑
             |
             |___→ v_river = 4 m/s
            /
           / v_resultant
          / (boat relative to ground)
```

**If boat moves perpendicular to river:**
|v_resultant| = √(10² + 4²) = √116 = 10.77 m/s at angle tan⁻¹(4/10) = 21.8° downstream

**If boat moves directly upriver (opposite to current):**
|v_resultant| = 10 - 4 = 6 m/s upstream

**Meaning:** The velocity relative to the ground is what an observer standing on the bank would measure. It's the combination of the boat's motion through water AND the water's motion.

### Q1.5: Resultant of Two Vectors [5 marks]
**Question:** Two forces of 12 N and 16 N act on a point at an angle of 60° to each other. Find (a) the magnitude of the resultant, (b) the angle it makes with the 12 N force, and (c) the angle it makes with the 16 N force.

**Solution:**
Given: F₁ = 12 N, F₂ = 16 N, θ = 60°

**(a) Magnitude of Resultant:**
R = √(F₁² + F₂² + 2F₁F₂ cosθ)
R = √(12² + 16² + 2(12)(16) cos60°)
R = √(144 + 256 + 2(12)(16)(0.5))
R = √(144 + 256 + 192)
R = √592 = **24.33 N**

**(b) Angle with 12 N force (α):**
Using sine rule: sin(α)/F₂ = sin(θ)/R
sin(α) = F₂ sin(θ) / R
sin(α) = 16 × sin(60°) / 24.33
sin(α) = 16 × 0.866 / 24.33
sin(α) = 13.856 / 24.33 = 0.569
**α = 34.7°**

**(c) Angle with 16 N force (β):**
β = θ - α = 60° - 34.7° = **25.3°**

Or using: sin(β)/F₁ = sin(θ)/R
sin(β) = 12 × sin(60°) / 24.33 = 0.427
**β = 25.3°**

### Q1.6: River-Boat Problem [5 marks]
**Question:** A boat capable of moving at 8 m/s in still water wants to cross a river of width 240 m. The current flows at 6 m/s. 
(a) In what direction should the boat head to cross perpendicular to the banks?
(b) How long will it take to cross?
(c) What is the resultant velocity of the boat?

**Solution:**

**(a) Direction to head perpendicular to banks:**
To go perpendicular, the boat's velocity component parallel to the current must equal and opposite to the current.

Let θ be the angle upstream from perpendicular.
- Perpendicular component needed: v_⊥ = √(8² - 6²) = √(64 - 36) = √28 = 5.29 m/s
- Parallel component (upstream): v_∥ = 6 m/s

sin(θ) = 6/8 = 0.75
**θ = 48.6° upstream from perpendicular** (or 41.4° from the bank)

**(b) Time to cross:**
The perpendicular component carries the boat across:
t = width / v_⊥ = 240 / 5.29 = **45.4 seconds**

**(c) Resultant velocity:**
Since boat crosses perpendicular, resultant velocity = perpendicular component = **5.29 m/s perpendicular to banks**

(The parallel components cancel, so the boat moves straight across)

### Q1.7: Relative Velocity - Two Objects [5 marks]
**Question:** A car A travels due East at 60 km/h. Car B travels due North at 80 km/h. Both pass through the same point. Find the velocity of car B relative to car A and the angle it makes.

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- **v_A** = 60 km/h due East
- **v_B** = 80 km/h due North

**Velocity of B relative to A:**
**v_BA** = **v_B** - **v_A**

In component form:
- **v_A** = 60**i** + 0**j** km/h
- **v_B** = 0**i** + 80**j** km/h
- **v_BA** = (0-60)**i** + (80-0)**j** = -60**i** + 80**j** km/h

**Magnitude:**
|**v_BA**| = √((-60)² + 80²) = √(3600 + 6400) = √10000 = **100 km/h**

**Direction:**
The velocity of B relative to A makes an angle with the negative x-axis (West direction):
tan(φ) = 80/60 = 4/3
φ = 53.1° **North of West** (or 126.9° from East)

**Interpretation:** From car A's perspective, car B appears to move at 100 km/h in a direction 53.1° North of West.

### Q2.1: Principle of Independence of Motion [3 marks]
**Question:** Explain the principle of independence of motion in projectile motion. Why can horizontal and vertical motions be analyzed separately?

**Answer:**
**Principle:** In projectile motion, the horizontal and vertical components of velocity are independent. The motion in one direction doesn't affect the motion in the perpendicular direction.

**Why they're independent:**

1. **No Acceleration Horizontally:** 
   - Neglecting air resistance, no horizontal force acts on the projectile
   - Horizontal acceleration = 0
   - Therefore, horizontal velocity remains constant throughout the flight

2. **Vertical Acceleration Only:**
   - Only gravity acts vertically (downward)
   - Vertical acceleration = -g (constant)
   - This doesn't affect horizontal motion

3. **Mathematical Independence:**
   - Horizontal: x = v_x × t (uniform motion equation)
   - Vertical: y = v_y₀ × t - ½gt² (uniformly accelerated motion)
   - Each equation is independent; t is the only common variable

4. **Parabolic Trajectory Result:**
   - Combining both equations (eliminating t) gives the trajectory equation:
   - y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)
   - This parabolic shape naturally emerges from independent linear and quadratic components

**Practical Example:** A ball rolled off a table:
- Horizontal velocity: constant
- Vertical velocity: increases due to gravity
- Both happen simultaneously but independently

### Q2.2: Maximum Height and Time of Flight [3 marks]
**Question:** Derive the expressions for maximum height and time of flight of a projectile launched at angle θ with initial velocity u.

**Answer:**
**Maximum Height Derivation:**

At maximum height, vertical velocity v_y = 0.
Using v_y² = u_y² - 2gH:
0 = (u sinθ)² - 2gH
**H = (u² sin²θ) / (2g)**

**Alternative using time:**
Time to reach max height: t_max = (u sinθ) / g
H = u sinθ × t_max - ½g(t_max)²
H = u sinθ × (u sinθ)/g - ½g × ((u sinθ)/g)²
**H = (u² sin²θ) / (2g)**

**Time of Flight Derivation:**

Total vertical displacement for complete flight = 0 (returns to launch height)
Using s = u_y × t - ½gt²:
0 = u sinθ × T - ½gT²
0 = T(u sinθ - ½gT)
T = 0 (initial) or **T = (2u sinθ) / g**

**Special Cases:**
- At 45°: Maximum range occurs. H = u²/(4g), T = (2u)/√2g = (√2 u)/g
- At 90°: Maximum height. H = u²/(2g), T = (2u)/g
- Time to reach half max height: t = (u sinθ)/(2g)

### Q2.3: Range of Projectile [3 marks]
**Question:** Show that the range of a projectile is maximum at 45° and why complementary angles (θ and 90°-θ) give the same range.

**Answer:**
**Range Derivation:**

Horizontal range R = horizontal velocity × time of flight
R = u cosθ × (2u sinθ)/g
**R = (u² sin2θ) / g**

**Maximum Range at 45°:**

R is maximum when sin2θ is maximum.
sin2θ is maximum when 2θ = 90°
Therefore, **θ = 45°**

R_max = u²/g (at 45°)

**Complementary Angles Give Same Range:**

For angles θ and (90° - θ):

R₁ = (u² sin2θ) / g
R₂ = (u² sin(2(90° - θ))) / g = (u² sin(180° - 2θ)) / g = (u² sin2θ) / g

Since sin(180° - 2θ) = sin(2θ):
**R₁ = R₂**

**Example:** 30° and 60° both give the same range:
- At 30°: sin(60°) = 0.866
- At 60°: sin(120°) = sin(180° - 120°) = sin(60°) = 0.866

**Physical Interpretation:**
- 30°: Low angle, higher speed component in horizontal direction, shorter flight time
- 60°: High angle, lower horizontal component, longer flight time
- These effects exactly compensate, giving equal ranges

**However, heights and trajectories differ:**
- 30° gives flatter trajectory, lower maximum height
- 60° gives steeper trajectory, higher maximum height, same range

### Q2.4: Basic Projectile Calculation [5 marks]
**Question:** A projectile is fired horizontally with initial velocity 40 m/s from the top of a cliff 80 m high. Find:
(a) Time of flight
(b) Horizontal range
(c) Velocity when it hits the ground
(d) Angle of velocity at impact

80 = ½ × 10 × t²
80 = 5t²

R = u_x × t = 40 × 4 = **160 m**

**(c) Final Velocity:**
Horizontal component (constant): v_x = 40 m/s
Vertical component: v_y = u_y + gt = 0 + 10 × 4 = 40 m/s

Magnitude: v = √(v_x² + v_y²) = √(40² + 40²) = √3200 = **40√2 = 56.57 m/s**

**(d) Angle at Impact:**
tan(α) = v_y / v_x = 40/40 = 1
**α = 45° below horizontal**

**Question:** A projectile is launched at 30° to the horizontal with initial speed 50 m/s. Find:
(a) Maximum height reached
(b) Time to reach maximum height
(c) Horizontal range
(d) Time of flight

**Solution:**

H = (u_y²) / (2g) = (25²) / (2 × 10) = 625 / 20 = **31.25 m**

t_max = u_y / g = 25 / 10 = **2.5 seconds**

**(c) Horizontal Range:**
R = (u² sin2θ) / g = (50² × sin(60°)) / 10 = (2500 × 0.866) / 10 = **216.5 m**

Or: R = u_x × T = 43.3 × 5 = 216.5 m ✓

**(d) Time of Flight:**
T = (2u sinθ) / g = (2 × 50 × sin(30°)) / 10 = (100 × 0.5) / 10 = **5 seconds**

### Q2.6: Projectile - Height and Range Problem [5 marks]
**Question:** A ball is projected at angle 37° to the horizontal with speed 20 m/s. A wall of height 5 m stands at horizontal distance 30 m. Will the ball clear the wall? If yes, by how much? (sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8, g = 10 m/s²)

**Solution:**

**Given:** θ = 37°, u = 20 m/s, wall height = 5 m, wall distance = 30 m

**Initial Components:**
- u_x = 20 × 0.8 = 16 m/s
- u_y = 20 × 0.6 = 12 m/s

**Time to reach wall (x = 30 m):**
x = u_x × t
30 = 16 × t
t = 1.875 s

**Height at wall:**
y = u_y × t - ½gt²
y = 12 × 1.875 - ½ × 10 × (1.875)²
y = 22.5 - 5 × 3.516
y = 22.5 - 17.58
y = **4.92 m**

**Analysis:**
Wall height = 5 m
Ball height at wall = 4.92 m

The ball reaches only 4.92 m, while the wall is 5 m high.
**The ball does NOT clear the wall. It falls short by 5 - 4.92 = 0.08 m = 8 cm**

Alternatively, if we check the trajectory equation:
y = x tanθ - (gx²)/(2u² cos²θ)
y = 30 × tan(37°) - (10 × 30²)/(2 × 20² × 0.8²)
y = 30 × 0.75 - 9000/(2 × 400 × 0.64)
y = 22.5 - 9000/512 = 22.5 - 17.58 = 4.92 m ✓

### Q2.7: Two-Stage Projectile Motion [5 marks]
**Question:** A cricketer throws a ball at 35° to the horizontal with initial speed 25 m/s. Another cricketer standing at horizontal distance 60 m wants to catch it at the same height from which it was thrown. At what height should he position his hands if he can catch a ball at heights from ground level up to 2.2 m?

Actual range for this projectile:

**Time to reach horizontal distance of 60 m:**
t = 60 / (u cosθ) = 60 / (25 × 0.819) = 60 / 20.475 = 2.93 s

# 3. WORK, ENERGY & MOMENTUM

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

### Q3.1: Work by Variable Force [3 marks]
**Question:** Define work done by a variable force. How is it different from constant force? Explain using a spring as an example.

**Answer:**
**Work by Constant Force:**
W = F · s = Fs cosθ
Work is simply force multiplied by displacement.

**Work by Variable Force:**
W = ∫ F(x) dx (from initial to final position)
The force changes with position, so we cannot use simple multiplication.

**Derivation for Spring:**
For a spring obeying Hooke's Law: F = -kx (restoring force)
When stretching the spring by distance x, work done against spring force:

W = ∫₀ˣ kx' dx' = k[x'²/2]₀ˣ = **½kx²**

**Physical Differences:**
1. **Graphical Interpretation:**
   - Constant force: W = F × s (rectangle under F-s graph)
   - Variable force: W = Area under F-s curve

2. **Spring Comparison:**
   - Initial force: 0 (at natural length)
   - Final force: kx (at extension x)
   - Average force: kx/2
   - Work = average force × displacement = (kx/2) × x = ½kx²

3. **Energy Stored:**
   - In spring, work done equals elastic potential energy stored
   - This energy is recoverable (when spring returns to natural length)

4. **Calculus Necessity:**
   - Constant force: Simple algebra
   - Variable force: Integration required because F changes continuously

**Real-world Examples:**
- Spring compression/extension: W = ½kx²
- Gravitational force (at large distances): W = GMm/r (integration of F = GMm/r²)
- Friction with varying normal force: W = ∫ μ(x)mg dx

---

### Q3.2: Work-Energy Theorem [3 marks]
**Question:** State and derive the work-energy theorem. Explain what it means physically.

**Answer:**
**Statement:** The net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy.
**W_net = ΔKE = KE_final - KE_initial**

**Derivation:**
Starting with Newton's second law:
F_net = ma

Taking dot product with displacement ds:
F_net · ds = ma · ds

Integrating from initial to final position:
∫ F_net · ds = ∫ ma · ds

Since a = dv/dt and ds = v dt:
W_net = ∫ m(dv/dt) × v dt = ∫ m v dv = m[v²/2]ᵤᵥ

**W_net = ½mv² - ½mu² = ½m(v² - u²)**

**Therefore: W_net = ΔKE** ✓

**Physical Meaning:**
1. **Positive Work:** W > 0 → ΔKE > 0 → Object speeds up
   (Force and displacement in similar directions)

2. **Negative Work:** W < 0 → ΔKE < 0 → Object slows down
   (Force opposes motion)

3. **Zero Work:** W = 0 → ΔKE = 0 → Constant speed
   (Force perpendicular to motion, like circular motion)

**Practical Applications:**
- **Braking:** Friction does negative work, reducing KE
- **Acceleration:** Engine force does positive work, increasing KE
- **Circular motion:** Centripetal force does zero work (perpendicular), speed stays constant
- **Incline:** Component of gravity along motion does work

**Advantage over Newton's Laws:**
- Newton: Requires knowledge of acceleration (complex for variable forces)
- Work-Energy: Only need initial and final velocities (simpler!)

### Q3.3: Impulse and Momentum [3 marks]
**Question:** Define impulse and explain how it relates to momentum change. Why do airbags in cars extend the time of impact?

**Answer:**
**Momentum (p):**
p = mv (product of mass and velocity)
Direction: same as velocity
It represents the "quantity of motion" of a body.

**Impulse (J):**
J = Δp = p_final - p_initial (change in momentum)
Also: J = F_avg × Δt (average force × time interval)

**Impulse-Momentum Theorem:**
J = F_avg × Δt = Δp = m(v_f - v_i)

Therefore: **F_avg = Δp / Δt = m(v_f - v_i) / Δt**

**Derivation:**
From Newton's second law: F = dp/dt
Integrating: ∫ F dt = ∫ dp = Δp
Therefore: J = ∫ F dt = Δp ✓

**Why Airbags Extend Impact Time:**

**Without Airbag:**
- Collision time: Δt₁ = very small (0.01 s)
- Momentum change: Δp = same (from v to 0)
- Required force: F₁ = Δp / Δt₁ = **VERY LARGE**
- Result: Person gets crushed by large impact force

**With Airbag:**
- Collision time: Δt₂ = larger (0.1 s) (airbag cushions gradually)
- Momentum change: Δp = same (from v to 0)
- Required force: F₂ = Δp / Δt₂ = **MUCH SMALLER**
- Result: Person experiences reduced force, survives impact

**Mathematical Comparison:**
If Δt₂ = 10 × Δt₁, then F₂ = F₁/10 (force reduces by 90%!)

**Other Examples:**
- Cricket fielder pulling arms back while catching (extends time)
- Gymnastics landing on mat vs concrete (mat extends time)
- Crumple zones in cars (extend deformation time, reduce peak force)

### Q3.4: Momentum Conservation in Collisions [3 marks]
**Question:** State the law of conservation of momentum. Explain why momentum is conserved in collisions but kinetic energy is not conserved in inelastic collisions.

**Answer:**
**Law of Conservation of Momentum:**
In an isolated system (no external forces), the total momentum before any collision equals the total momentum after the collision.

**p_before = p_after**
**m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂**

**Why Momentum is Always Conserved in Collisions:**

The collision forces are **internal forces** between the two objects.
- By Newton's 3rd Law: F₁₂ = -F₂₁ (equal and opposite)
- Impulse on object 1: J₁ = -Impulse on object 2: J₂
- Change in p₁ = -Change in p₂
- Therefore: Δ(m₁u₁ + m₂u₂) = 0
- **Total momentum unchanged** ✓

External forces (air resistance, friction with ground) are negligible during brief collision.

**Why Kinetic Energy is NOT Conserved in Inelastic Collisions:**

**Elastic Collision (KE Conserved):**
- No deformation of objects
- All kinetic energy converted back to motion
- ½m₁u₁² + ½m₂u₂² = ½m₁v₁² + ½m₂v₂²
- Example: Billiard balls colliding

**Inelastic Collision (KE NOT Conserved):**
- Objects deform, stick together, or produce sound
- Some kinetic energy converts to other forms:
  - Heat energy
  - Sound energy
  - Deformation/strain energy
  - Internal energy

**Perfectly Inelastic Collision:**
Objects stick together after collision: v₁ = v₂ = v_common

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v_common

KE_before = ½m₁u₁² + ½m₂u₂²
KE_after = ½(m₁ + m₂)v²_common

**KE_after < KE_before** (energy "lost" to heat, sound, deformation)

**Energy "Loss" Calculation:**
ΔKE = KE_after - KE_before < 0 (negative means energy lost)

This "lost" energy becomes internal energy, heat, etc.

**Key Point:** Momentum is ALWAYS conserved (vector law), but mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved only in elastic processes. Inelastic processes dissipate mechanical energy.

### Q3.5: Work by Variable Force [5 marks]
**Question:** A force F = 3x + 2 (in Newtons, where x is in meters) acts on a 2 kg object. Find the work done when the object moves from x = 0 to x = 4 m. Also find the final velocity if it starts from rest.

**Solution:**

**Given:** F(x) = 3x + 2, m = 2 kg, initial position = 0, final position = 4 m, u = 0

**(a) Work Done:**
W = ∫₀⁴ F(x) dx = ∫₀⁴ (3x + 2) dx

W = [3x²/2 + 2x]₀⁴

W = [3(4)²/2 + 2(4)] - [0]

W = [3(16)/2 + 8] = [24 + 8]

**W = 32 Joules**

**(b) Final Velocity:**
Using work-energy theorem: W = ΔKE = ½mv² - ½mu²

32 = ½(2)v² - 0

32 = v²

**v = √32 = 4√2 = 5.66 m/s**

**Verification using integration:**
Using F = ma = m(dv/dt):
3x + 2 = 2(dv/dt)

Using dv/dt = v(dv/dx):
3x + 2 = 2v(dv/dx)

(3x + 2)dx = 2v dv

Integrating both sides:

### Q4.1: Moment of Inertia [3 marks]
**Question:** Define moment of inertia and explain how it's different from mass. Derive the moment of inertia of a uniform rod about one end.

**Answer:**
**Moment of Inertia (I) Definition:**
I = Σ mᵢrᵢ² (for discrete masses)
I = ∫ r² dm (for continuous distribution)

It represents the rotational inertia—the resistance of an object to change in rotational motion.
Units: kg·m²

**Difference from Mass:**
- **Mass (m):** Resistance to linear acceleration (F = ma)
- **Moment of Inertia (I):** Resistance to angular acceleration (τ = Iα)
- Both are properties of resistance, but to different types of motion
- I depends on mass distribution (axis dependent), m doesn't

**Derivation for Uniform Rod About One End:**

Consider a uniform rod of length L and mass M.
Take the end as origin, x-axis along rod.

**Mass element:** dm = (M/L) dx (uniform density)

**Distance from axis:** r = x

**Moment of Inertia:**
I = ∫ r² dm = ∫₀ᴸ x² (M/L) dx

I = (M/L) ∫₀ᴸ x² dx

I = (M/L) [x³/3]₀ᴸ

I = (M/L) × (L³/3)

**I = (1/3) ML²**

**Comparison with Other Axes:**
- About center: I_center = (1/12) ML²
- About one end: I_end = (1/3) ML² = 4 × I_center

**Why? **At the end, most mass is farther from axis, hence larger I.

### Q4.3: Geostationary Satellites [3 marks]
**Question:** What is a geostationary satellite? Explain the three conditions required for a satellite to be geostationary. Why is the orbital radius always 42,000 km?

**Answer:**
**Definition:** A geostationary satellite appears stationary relative to Earth—it remains over the same point on the equator throughout its orbit.

**Three Essential Conditions:**

1) **Orbital Period = Earth's Rotation Period**
   - T = 24 hours = 86,400 seconds
   - If T ≠ 24 hours, satellite will drift relative to Earth's surface

2) **Orbit Above Equator**
   - The orbital plane must coincide with Earth's equatorial plane
   - Any other orbital plane would result in the satellite moving North-South relative to ground

3) **Same Direction as Earth's Rotation**
   - West-to-East (counterclockwise from North Pole view)
   - Must revolve in same direction as Earth, not against it

**Why Orbital Radius is Always 42,000 km:**

For circular orbit, gravitational force provides centripetal force:
GMm/r² = mω²r = m(2π/T)²r

GM/r² = (4π²/T²)r

r³ = (GMT²)/(4π²)

**r = ∛[GMT²/(4π²)]**

Substituting values:
- G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
- M = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg (Earth's mass)
- T = 86,400 s

r³ = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ × 86400²) / (4π²)
r³ ≈ 7.496 × 10²² m³
**r ≈ 4.22 × 10⁷ m = 42,200 km** (from Earth's center)

**Height above surface:**
h = r - R_Earth = 42,200 - 6,371 = **35,829 km ≈ 36,000 km**

**This orbital radius is UNIQUE and FIXED** because:
- Increase T → need larger r (farther away)
- Decrease T → need smaller r (closer)
- Only one r satisfies T = 24 hours

**Applications of Geostationary Satellites:**
- Telecommunications
- Weather monitoring
- TV broadcasting
- Must always stay over same spot

### Q5.1: Simple Harmonic Motion Definition [3 marks]
**Question:** Define simple harmonic motion (SHM). What is the condition for SHM? Derive the equation of motion for SHM.

**Answer:**
**Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) Definition:**
SHM is periodic motion where the restoring force (or acceleration) is directly proportional to the displacement from equilibrium and always directed toward equilibrium.

**Condition for SHM:**
F = -kx (or a = -ω²x)

Where:
- F is the restoring force
- x is displacement from equilibrium
- k is a constant (spring constant)
- Negative sign indicates force opposes displacement

**Equation of Motion Derivation:**

Newton's second law: F = ma
-kx = m(d²x/dt²)
d²x/dt² + (k/m)x = 0

Let ω² = k/m (ω is angular frequency)

**d²x/dt² + ω²x = 0** (Differential equation of SHM)

**Solution:**
x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ)

Or: x(t) = A cos(ωt) + B sin(ωt)

Where:
- A is amplitude (maximum displacement)
- ω is angular frequency
- φ is phase constant
- T = 2π/ω is period
- f = ω/2π is frequency

**Properties of SHM:**
1. Acceleration: a(t) = -ω²A cos(ωt + φ) = -ω²x
2. Velocity: v(t) = -ωA sin(ωt + φ)
3. Maximum velocity: v_max = ωA (at equilibrium)
4. Maximum acceleration: a_max = ω²A (at extreme positions)
5. Period is independent of amplitude
6. Energy oscillates between potential and kinetic

### Q5.2: Energy in SHM [3 marks]
**Question:** Show that the total energy in SHM is constant. How are kinetic and potential energies distributed?

**Answer:**
**Energy in SHM:**

For SHM: x(t) = A cos(ωt)
v(t) = dx/dt = -Aω sin(ωt)

**Potential Energy:**
U(t) = ½kx² = ½mω²x²
U(t) = ½mω²A² cos²(ωt)

**Kinetic Energy:**
K(t) = ½mv² = ½m(-Aω sin(ωt))²
K(t) = ½mω²A² sin²(ωt)

**Total Energy:**
E_total = K + U
E_total = ½mω²A² sin²(ωt) + ½mω²A² cos²(ωt)
E_total = ½mω²A²[sin²(ωt) + cos²(ωt)]
**E_total = ½mω²A² = ½kA²** (constant!)

**Energy Distribution:**

At different positions:
1. **At Equilibrium (x = 0):**
   - U = 0 (all energy is kinetic)
   - K = ½mω²A² = E_total
   - v is maximum

2. **At Amplitude (x = ±A):**
   - U = ½kA² = E_total (all energy is potential)
   - K = 0
   - v = 0 (momentary rest)

3. **At x = A/2:**
   - U = ½k(A/2)² = ⅛kA²
   - K = ½kA² - ⅛kA² = ⅜kA²
   - K:U ratio = 3:1

**Energy Oscillation Frequency:**
E_oscillates with twice the frequency of motion (SHM frequency = f, energy oscillation = 2f)

**Graphical Representation:**
```
Energy
   |     Total Energy = constant
   |     /‾‾\     /‾‾\
   |    /    \   /    \  (KE oscillates)
   |   /      \_/      \
   |  /   (PE oscillates)
   |_/_________________(KE+PE)
   +--+--+--+--+--+--+--
      At x=0  At x=±A
```

# 6. WAVE OPTICS (INTERFERENCE & DIFFRACTION)

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

### Q6.1: Interference Condition [3 marks]
**Question:** What are coherent sources? State the conditions for constructive and destructive interference. Why are ordinary light sources incoherent?

**Answer:**
**Coherent Sources:**
Sources are coherent if they maintain a constant phase difference and have the same frequency. Waves from coherent sources can produce stable interference patterns.

**Conditions for Interference:**

**Constructive Interference (Bright Fringes):**
- Path difference: Δ = mλ (where m = 0, 1, 2, 3...)
- Resultant intensity: I_max = (√I₁ + √I₂)²
- For equal intensities I₁ = I₂ = I: I_max = 4I (intensity quadruples!)

**Destructive Interference (Dark Fringes):**
- Path difference: Δ = (m + ½)λ (where m = 0, 1, 2...)
- Resultant intensity: I_min = (√I₁ - √I₂)²
- For equal intensities: I_min = 0 (complete cancellation possible!)

**Why Ordinary Light is Incoherent:**

1. **Ordinary Sources Emit Randomly:**
   - Atoms emit light independently
   - Each atom emits for ~10⁻⁸ seconds (coherence time)
   - After this, the phase changes randomly

2. **No Fixed Phase Relationship:**
   - Multiple atoms don't maintain constant phase difference
   - Phase difference changes randomly and rapidly
   - Average phase difference is random

3. **Incoherent Light Characteristics:**
   - Cannot produce stable interference patterns with another incoherent source
   - If you try double-slit with two separate lamps, no interference pattern forms
   - Pattern keeps changing randomly due to phase fluctuations

4. **Why Laser Light is Different:**
   - Stimulated emission produces coherent waves
   - All waves have same phase and frequency
   - Constant phase relationship maintained
   - Produces clear, stable interference patterns

**Modern Coherent Sources:**
- Lasers (most coherent)
- LED with filters
- Single slit (coherent portions of ordinary light)

---

### Q6.2: Diffraction vs Interference [3 marks]
**Question:** Distinguish between interference and diffraction. Why does diffraction occur? Can an object have both interference and diffraction simultaneously?

**Answer:**
**Interference:**
- Superposition of waves from two (or more) **separate coherent sources**
- Waves combine: I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂)cosδ
- Results in regular pattern of bright and dark bands
- Requires coherence between sources

**Diffraction:**
- Bending of waves around obstacles or through apertures
- Wave from **single source** is modified by an obstacle/aperture
- Results in spreading of waves
- Doesn't require coherence (occurs even with single source)

**Key Differences:**

| Feature | Interference | Diffraction |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| **Number of sources** | Two or more coherent sources | Single source, one wavefront |
| **Requirement** | Coherence needed | No coherence needed |
| **Pattern** | Multiple sharp fringes | Central max + side lobes |
| **Order of pattern** | Uniform spacing | Non-uniform intensity |
| **Obstruction** | Not essential | Essential (slit/obstacle) |

**Why Diffraction Occurs:**

According to Huygens' Principle, each point on a wavefront becomes a secondary source.
When a wavefront passes through a slit or around an obstacle:
- Parts of the wavefront are blocked
- Remaining parts diffract (spread) into the shadow region
- Wavelets spread beyond the geometric shadow

**Can Both Occur Together?**

**YES! This is actually common:**

**Example: Double-Slit Interference Pattern**
1. Light from single source passes through two slits
2. **Diffraction:** Each slit acts as a secondary source. Light diffracts through each slit
3. **Interference:** Diffracted light from slit 1 interferes with diffracted light from slit 2
4. **Result:** Fringes, BUT with varying intensity!

**Why Intensity Varies:**
- Central region: Both slits contribute (interference fringes bright)
- Sides: Diffraction from individual slits reduces (fringes dim)
- Far sides: Diffraction maxima of one slit aligns with diffraction minima of other slit

**The Combined Pattern:**
- Interference sets fringe spacing (λD/d)
- Diffraction sets overall intensity envelope
- Central bright fringes are bright
- Higher order fringes fade (diffraction dimming)

(b) Angle of the second diffraction minimum

# 7. GEOMETRICAL OPTICS (LENSES & MIRRORS)

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

### Q7.1: Lens Formula Derivation [3 marks]
**Question:** Derive the lens formula. Explain the sign conventions used.

**Answer:**
**Lens Formula Derivation:**

Consider a convex lens of focal length f forming an image of an object.
- Object distance: u (from lens)
- Image distance: v (from lens)
- Object height: h
- Image height: h'

Using similar triangles in the ray diagram:

From the right triangle (object side):
h/u = (focal point to image height)/f

From similar triangles (image side):
h'/v = (focal point to object height)/f

**Combining geometry:**
Through triangle similarity and algebraic manipulation:

1/u + 1/v = 1/f

**This is the Lens Formula: 1/f = 1/u + 1/v**

Or equivalently: **1/f = 1/u + 1/v** (Newton form)

**Alternative forms:**
- v = uf/(u-f)
- u = vf/(v-f)

**Sign Conventions (Cartesian):**

1. **Distances measured from lens:**
   - (+) to the right of lens
   - (-) to the left of lens

2. **Object distance (u):**
   - **Always negative** (objects are in front of lens)
   - u < 0 for real objects

3. **Image distance (v):**
   - Negative for real images (on opposite side, below axis)
   - Positive for virtual images (same side as object, above axis)

4. **Focal length (f):**
   - Positive for convex lens (converging)
   - Negative for concave lens (diverging)

5. **Heights (object & image):**
   - Positive above principal axis
   - Negative below principal axis

**Magnification:**
m = -v/u = h'/h

- m < 0: Image is inverted
- m > 0: Image is erect
- |m| > 1: Image is enlarged
- |m| < 1: Image is diminished

### Q7.2: Mirror Formula and Types of Images [3 marks]
**Question:** Derive the mirror formula and compare the images formed by concave and convex mirrors.

**Answer:**
**Mirror Formula Derivation:**

Using same geometry and similar triangles as lens formula:

**Mirror Formula: 1/f = 1/u + 1/v**

(Same as lens formula mathematically!)

**Sign Conventions for Mirrors:**

1. **Distances from mirror pole:**
   - Positive in front of mirror (real)
   - Negative behind mirror (virtual)

2. **Focal length:**
   - Positive for concave mirror (converging)
   - Negative for convex mirror (diverging)

3. **Center of curvature:** C = 2f (behind for concave, in front for convex)

**Concave Mirror Images:**

| Object Position | Image | Type | Size | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beyond C (u > 2f) | Between f and C | Real | Diminished | Inverted |
| At C (u = 2f) | At C (v = 2f) | Real | Equal | Inverted |
| Between C and f (f < u < 2f) | Beyond C | Real | Enlarged | Inverted |
| At f (u = f) | At ∞ | Real | ∞ | Inverted |
| Between f and P (u < f) | Behind mirror | Virtual | Enlarged | Erect |

**Concave mirror characteristics:**
- Real, inverted images when u > f
- Virtual, erect images when u < f (acts as magnifying glass)

**Convex Mirror Images:**

For all object positions:
- Image is always **virtual**
- Image is always **erect**
- Image is always **diminished** (|m| < 1)
- Image always lies between pole and focus

**Why?**
- Focal length is negative (f < 0)
- Using mirror formula: v = uf/(u-f) → always v < 0 (behind mirror)
- Magnification m = -v/u → always positive (erect)

**Convex mirror applications:**
- Rear-view mirrors in vehicles (wide field of view, diminished images)
- Shop security mirrors (see wide area)

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

### Q8.1: Bernoulli's Principle [3 marks]
**Question:** State Bernoulli's principle and derive Bernoulli's equation. Explain its assumptions.

**Answer:**
**Bernoulli's Principle:**
For an ideal fluid flowing along a streamline, the sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy per unit volume remains constant.

**Bernoulli's Equation:**
P + ½ρv² + ρgh = **constant** (along a streamline)

Or, between two points:
P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂

**With head terms:**
(P/ρg) + (v²/2g) + h = **constant**

Where:
- P/ρg = pressure head
- v²/2g = velocity head
- h = gravitational head

**Derivation:**

Consider fluid element moving along streamline.
Work-energy theorem:
W_total = ΔKE + ΔPE

Work by pressure forces:
W_pressure = P₁A₁L₁ - P₂A₂L₂

Using continuity: A₁L₁ = A₂L₂ = ΔV (volume)
W_pressure = (P₁ - P₂)ΔV

Change in KE:
ΔKE = ½m(v₂² - v₁²) = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²)

Change in PE:
ΔPE = mgΔh = ρgΔVΔh

Applying work-energy theorem:
(P₁ - P₂)ΔV = ½ρΔV(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔVΔh

Dividing by ΔV:
P₁ - P₂ = ½ρ(v₂² - v₁²) + ρgΔh

Rearranging:
**P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂** ✓

**Assumptions:**

1. **Incompressible fluid:** Density ρ is constant
2. **Non-viscous:** No friction/viscous forces
3. **Steady flow:** Velocity at any point doesn't change with time
4. **Along a streamline:** Valid only for points on same streamline
5. **No external work:** No pumps or turbines

**Physical Meaning:**

Where velocity is high → pressure is low (and vice versa)

This explains:
- Airplane wing lift (fast air over top = lower pressure)
- Water jet from pipe narrows (higher velocity in narrower region)
- Venturi effect (pressure decreases in narrow section)

---

### Q8.2: Continuity Equation [3 marks]
**Question:** Derive the continuity equation. Show that A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ for incompressible fluid flow.

**Answer:**
**Continuity Equation (Conservation of Mass):**

For incompressible, steady flow through a pipe:
**A₁v₁ = A₂v₂** (mass flow rate is constant)

Or: **A·v = constant** (volumetric flow rate is constant)

**Derivation:**

Consider a pipe with varying cross-section.
In time dt, fluid travels distances:
- At section 1: L₁ = v₁ dt
- At section 2: L₂ = v₂ dt

**Volume entering at section 1:**
V₁ = A₁ × L₁ = A₁ × v₁ × dt

**Volume leaving at section 2:**
V₂ = A₂ × L₂ = A₂ × v₂ × dt

For incompressible fluid (ρ = constant):
**Mass entering = Mass leaving**

ρA₁v₁dt = ρA₂v₂dt

Canceling ρdt:
**A₁v₁ = A₂v₂** ✓

**Volumetric Flow Rate:**
Q = A × v = constant

Units: m³/s

**Physical Interpretation:**

When pipe narrows:
- A decreases → v must increase
- Faster flow in narrow sections
- Example: Water spraying far from a nozzle (narrower opening → faster water)

When pipe widens:
- A increases → v must decrease
- Slower flow in wide sections

**Combined with Bernoulli's Equation:**

P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

In narrow section:
- v is large (from continuity)
- P must be small (from Bernoulli)
- This is why pressure drops in narrow sections

**Practical Applications:**
1. **Carburetors:** Narrow passages create low pressure to draw fuel
2. **Airplane wings:** Curved top surface accelerates air (low pressure = lift)
3. **Water meters:** Constriction increases velocity, pressure drop indicates flow

### Q8.3: Bernoulli's Equation Application [5 marks]
**Question:** Water flows through a horizontal pipe that narrows from diameter 10 cm to 5 cm. The pressure at the wide section is 50 kPa and velocity is 1 m/s. Find:
(a) Velocity in narrow section
(b) Pressure in narrow section
(c) Volumetric flow rate
(d) Why does pressure decrease in the narrow section?

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- Wide section: D₁ = 10 cm, r₁ = 5 cm, P₁ = 50 kPa = 50,000 Pa, v₁ = 1 m/s
- Narrow section: D₂ = 5 cm, r₂ = 2.5 cm
- ρ = 1000 kg/m³ (water)

**(a) Velocity in Narrow Section:**

Using continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

A₁ = πr₁² = π(0.05)² = 0.00785 m²
A₂ = πr₂² = π(0.025)² = 0.00196 m²

v₂ = A₁v₁/A₂ = (0.00785 × 1)/0.00196
v₂ = 0.00785/0.00196 = **4 m/s**

Check: (0.05/0.025)² = 2² = 4 (area ratio inverse = velocity ratio) ✓

**(b) Pressure in Narrow Section:**

Using Bernoulli's equation (horizontal pipe, h₁ = h₂):
P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²

P₂ = P₁ + ½ρ(v₁² - v₂²)
P₂ = 50,000 + ½(1000)(1² - 4²)
P₂ = 50,000 + 500(1 - 16)
P₂ = 50,000 + 500(-15)
P₂ = 50,000 - 7,500
**P₂ = 42,500 Pa = 42.5 kPa**

**Pressure drops by 7.5 kPa (15% drop)**

Q = A₁v₁ = 0.00785 × 1 = **0.00785 m³/s = 7.85 L/s**

Or: Q = A₂v₂ = 0.00196 × 4 = 0.00784 ≈ 0.00785 m³/s ✓

**(d) Why Pressure Decreases:**

From Bernoulli's principle:
- As water enters narrow section, it must speed up (continuity)
- Higher speed → higher kinetic energy (½ρv²)
- Total energy must be constant
- Therefore, pressure energy (P) must decrease
- **Pressure converts to kinetic energy**

This is characteristic of Bernoulli's principle:
- Pressure head + Velocity head + Elevation head = constant
- High velocity region has low pressure
- Low velocity region has high pressure

# 9. THERMODYNAMICS

## A. THEORETICAL QUESTIONS (3 marks each)

### Q9.1: First Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]
**Question:** State and explain the first law of thermodynamics. Define internal energy, heat, and work.

**Answer:**
**First Law of Thermodynamics:**
The heat energy supplied to a system equals the sum of the change in internal energy and the work done by the system.

**ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW**

Or: **ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV** (for constant pressure)

**Definitions:**

1. **Internal Energy (U):**
   - Total kinetic and potential energy of all molecules in the system
   - Depends only on temperature (for ideal gas)
   - State function (depends only on current state, not path)
   - Increases with temperature

2. **Heat (Q):**
   - Energy transferred due to temperature difference
   - **Positive (Q > 0):** Heat absorbed by system
   - **Negative (Q < 0):** Heat released by system
   - Path function (depends on how energy is transferred)

3. **Work (W):**
   - Energy transferred by mechanical means (expansion/compression)
   - **Positive (W > 0):** Work done BY system (expansion)
   - **Negative (W < 0):** Work done ON system (compression)
   - W = PΔV for constant pressure

**First Law Interpretation:**

ΔQ = ΔU + W
- Heat input = Increase in internal energy + Work done by system
- **Energy conservation:** Total energy input must equal total output

**Example Processes:**

1. **Isothermal (T = constant):**
   - ΔU = 0 (temperature constant)
   - ΔQ = W (all heat converts to work)

2. **Adiabatic (ΔQ = 0):**
   - 0 = ΔU + W
   - ΔU = -W (work done reduces internal energy, temperature drops)

3. **Isochoric (V = constant):**
   - W = 0 (no expansion)
   - ΔQ = ΔU (all heat increases internal energy)

4. **Isobaric (P = constant):**
   - W = PΔV
   - ΔQ = ΔU + PΔV

---

### Q9.2: Second Law of Thermodynamics [3 marks]
**Question:** State the second law of thermodynamics in two forms. Explain why perfect heat engines are impossible.

**Answer:**
**Second Law - Kelvin-Planck Statement:**
It is impossible to construct an engine that operates in a cycle, producing only work and absorbing energy from a single thermal reservoir. Some heat must be rejected to a cold reservoir.

**Implication:** Heat cannot be completely converted to work in a cyclic process.

**Second Law - Clausius Statement:**
It is impossible for heat to flow spontaneously from a cold body to a hot body without external work being done.

**Implication:** Heat flows naturally from hot to cold, never spontaneously reverse.

**Why Perfect Heat Engines Are Impossible:**

A "perfect" engine would:
- Take heat Q_h from hot reservoir (at temperature T_h)
- Convert ALL of it to useful work (W = Q_h)
- Reject NO heat to cold reservoir (Q_c = 0)

**This violates the second law** because:
1. **Energy alone isn't sufficient:**
   - First law doesn't forbid this (ΔU = 0 for cycle)
   - Energy is conserved, but it's thermodynamically forbidden

2. **Entropy Argument:**
   - Heat extraction decreases hot reservoir's entropy: ΔS_h = -Q_h/T_h
   - No heat added to cold reservoir: ΔS_c = 0
   - Total entropy: ΔS_total = -Q_h/T_h < 0
   - **Entropy decreases, violating second law!**
   - Second law requires: ΔS_total ≥ 0

3. **Maximum Efficiency (Carnot Limit):**
Actual efficiency: η = W/Q_h = (Q_h - Q_c)/Q_h = 1 - (Q_c/Q_h)

For Carnot engine (idealized, reversible):
η_Carnot = 1 - T_c/T_h

Even this perfect engine cannot achieve 100% efficiency:
- If T_c = 0 K: η = 100% (only theoretically)
- In practice: T_c > 0, so η < 1

**Why All Real Engines Are Less Efficient:**
1. Friction and dissipation
2. Irreversible processes
3. Non-ideal heat transfer
4. Pressure/temperature losses

### Q10.1: Standing Waves on a String [3 marks]
**Question:** Explain how standing waves are formed on a stretched string. Define nodes and antinodes. What is the formula for the speed of a transverse wave on a string?

**Answer:**
**Formation of Standing Waves:**
Standing waves are formed when two identical waves traveling in opposite directions superpose (interfere). On a stretched string fixed at both ends, the incident wave reflects from the fixed ends and interferes with the incoming wave.

**Nodes and Antinodes:**
- **Nodes:** Points of zero displacement (amplitude = 0). They occur where destructive interference is complete. At fixed ends, nodes always form.
- **Antinodes:** Points of maximum displacement (amplitude = max). They occur where constructive interference is complete.

**Wave Speed Formula:**
The speed of a transverse wave on a stretched string is given by:
**v = √(T/μ)**

Where:
- v = wave speed (m/s)
- T = tension in the string (N)
- μ = linear mass density (mass per unit length, kg/m)

**Harmonics:**
The string vibrates in natural modes called harmonics.
Length L = n(λ/2), where n = 1, 2, 3...
Frequency f_n = n(v/2L)

### Q10.2: Doppler Effect [3 marks]
**Question:** Define the Doppler effect. Giving the general formula for apparent frequency, explain how frequency changes when a source approaches an observer.

**Answer:**
**Definition:**
The Doppler effect is the apparent change in frequency of a wave detected by an observer because of relative motion between the source of the wave and the observer.

**General Formula:**
**f' = f [(v ± v_o) / (v ∓ v_s)]**

Where:
- f' = apparent frequency
- f = actual source frequency
- v = speed of sound in the medium
- v_o = speed of observer
- v_s = speed of source

**Sign Convention (Source Approaching Observer):**
- Observer is stationary (v_o = 0)
- Source moves towards observer (v_s is positive in direction of sound, but effectively reduces wavelength)
- Formula becomes: f' = f [v / (v - v_s)]
- Since denominator (v - v_s) is smaller than v, the ratio is > 1.
- Therefore, **f' > f** (Frequency increases).
- Pitch appears higher.

**(b) Fundamental Frequency (n=1):**
For fundamental mode, L = λ/2 → λ = 2L
f₁ = v / λ = v / 2L
f₁ = 316.2 / (2 × 0.8)
f₁ = 316.2 / 1.6
**f₁ = 197.6 Hz**

**(c) Third Harmonic (n=3):**
f₃ = 3 × f₁
f₃ = 3 × 197.6
**f₃ = 592.8 Hz**

(This produces a higher pitch note, an overtone)

### Q10.4: Doppler Effect Calculation [5 marks]
**Question:** A train blowing its whistle at frequency 400 Hz approaches a stationary observer on the platform at a speed of 36 km/h. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.
(a) What is the apparent frequency heard by the observer as the train approaches?
(b) What is the apparent frequency heard after the train passes the observer?
(c) What is the change in frequency?

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- f = 400 Hz
- v_s = 36 km/h = 36 × (5/18) = **10 m/s**
- v_o = 0 (observer stationary)
- v = 340 m/s

**(a) Train Approaching:**
Formula: f' = f [v / (v - v_s)]
f_approach = 400 [340 / (340 - 10)]
f_approach = 400 [340 / 330]
f_approach = 400 × 1.0303
**f_approach = 412.1 Hz**

**(b) Train Receding (Moving Away):**
Formula: f'' = f [v / (v + v_s)]
f_recede = 400 [340 / (340 + 10)]
f_recede = 400 [340 / 350]
f_recede = 400 × 0.9714
**f_recede = 388.6 Hz**

**(c) Change in Frequency:**
Δf = f_approach - f_recede
Δf = 412.1 - 388.6
**Δf = 23.5 Hz**

(This sudden drop in pitch is characteristic of the Doppler effect on passing)
